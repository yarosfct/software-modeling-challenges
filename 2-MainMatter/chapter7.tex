\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{cha:conclusion}

This thesis asked how software modelling is experienced, taught, and learned in higher education, treating modelling as a socio-technical activity rather than as notation drill alone. Fourteen interviews (four lecturers, ten students) were analysed with a lightweight STGT procedure: open coding, short memos, constant comparison, and a findings map. The result is an empirically grounded account of \textbf{14 categories} and \textbf{four phenomena}, sufficient for the research questions of this MSc. The four phenomena in Chapter~\ref{cha:findings} are claims that several categories support together (Figures~\ref{fig:findings_map} and~\ref{fig:relations_map}). PH1 is the first diagram. PH2, PH3, and PH4 bring together strategies, support needs, and course conditions.

\section{Answers to the research questions}
\label{sec:conc_rqs}

On RQ1, the main challenges are a gated first modelling move, symbol and framework overload as a distinct problem, difficulty judging ``good enough'' when most of the modelling tools the participants described did not compile or simulate the model, and breakdowns when students switch notation families. A compile check would show that a model is well-formed, and would leave open whether the diagram says what the person who made it meant.

On RQ2, worked examples and guided walkthroughs remain a main way the work starts. L4 guides students as they solve the exercise, rather than solving it for them first. Students also described their own procedures for getting a first diagram started. The interviews do not show that these courses taught no process. Feedback is both late and unequally accessible. Where students used AI, they asked it to explain or compare, and S5 also asked for alternative class diagrams and then rewrote the graded model rather than handing in the generated output.

On RQ3, semantic checks are wanted, and a heavy or optional tool may still be dropped. A group can keep a shared diagram or abandon it. People take more care when someone will actually read the model. Heavy workloads and continuous assessment leave less time for worked examples and for feedback while the model can still change. L1 said classes are not long enough to cover every example, and that students sometimes skip a class to study for a test.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:conc_contributions}

The contribution is a bounded, traceable map of modelling-education challenges and support needs that connects student and lecturer voices to the socio-technical conditions of courses, tools, groups, and feedback. Relative to method-specific error taxonomies and Bloom-based learning-outcome frameworks, the account splits mechanisms that are often collapsed (start-gate versus symbol overload; feedback timing versus access; checking wanted versus tools dropped) and treats family translation as its own challenge. Chapter~\ref{cha:discussion} reads these patterns as possible changes to courses and tools. This study did not test whether those changes would help. The interviews do not show that lecturers fail to teach, comment, or relate one language to another. A comment can still leave the model unchanged. A student can still miss how the next language relates to one they already know.

\section{Limits}
\label{sec:conc_limits}

Interviewing stopped after the four further interviews. They did not add a 15th overview category. The 14 categories were judged sufficient for the research questions of this thesis. Sims and Cilliers call that theoretical sufficiency \cite{sims2023qualitatively}. Further properties could still appear. The second round was sought because open questions remained. The students who took part had done software modelling and were available. They were not chosen one by one to match each open question. The limit on arranging further interviews is stated in Section~\ref{sec:method_rigour}. No further lecturers were interviewed. Several open questions remain labelled for later work (Table~\ref{tab:open_questions}). What transfers is the set of conditions under which a category appeared.

\section{Closing}
\label{sec:conc_closing}

Future work can take up three questions this thesis leaves open. One is whether ``students wait to start'' describes a whole class or only some students in it. That would need the lecturer and the students from the same course, or a look at the first minutes of a lab. Another is whether, in a course that uses more than one language, students were shown how the next language relates to the previous one before they had to move from one language to the other. A third is whether students, in their own words, describe the difficulty of deciding what a model should leave out. Lecturers in this study described that difficulty. Students did not. The detail is in Section~\ref{sec:disc_future}. Those questions are a different project from the account this thesis has already produced.
