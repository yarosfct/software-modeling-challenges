# Teacher Interview Guide C — Gap-Filling Version (25–30 min)

This version extends Guide B ([pilot-teacher-focused.md](pilot-teacher-focused.md)) with probes for themes under-represented in the current lecturer corpus: multi-family transitions, scale-dependent pedagogy, shy/passive students, AI and assessment change, integrated tooling, and requirements/socio-technical teaching.

**Use when:** you want interviews that strengthen cross-case claims on RQ2/RQ3, especially AI, scale, collaboration, and RE-oriented practice.

---

## 0. Opening (30–45s)

**What you say**

> Thanks for meeting with me. I am studying how modelling is taught and learned in higher education, especially the difficulties students face, the kinds of support that help them, and how teaching choices, tools, and course conditions shape that experience.
> With your permission, I would like to record so I do not miss details. This should take around 25 to 30 minutes. You can skip any question.

*(If not recording: "I will take notes instead.")*

---

## 1. Fast setup (1–2 min)

### Q1 — Minimal teaching context

**Ask**

* "To situate your answers, could you briefly tell me what modelling-related course or courses you teach, which modelling families you cover, and roughly how large your classes are?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Introductory or advanced?"
* "How long have you been teaching this content?"
* "Individual work, groups, or a mix?"

---

## 2. Competence and assessment (4–5 min)

### Q2 — Intended competence and assessment alignment

**Ask**

* "By the end of your course, what should students be able to do with modelling, and where in the course do you actually see whether they can do it?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Which activities matter most: interpreting, creating, refining, evaluating?"
* "What does a merely acceptable model look like, compared with a strong one?"
* "Is there anything you care about in modelling that your current assessment does not capture well?"

### Q3 — Process from problem to model

**Ask**

* "When students face a new modelling task, what process do you expect them to follow from the problem statement to a first workable model?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Do you teach that process explicitly?"
* "Where do they most often break down: getting started, abstraction, semantics, refinement, or quality checking?"
* "Are there misunderstandings that recur across different notations?"

---

## 3. Concrete evidence (5–6 min)

### Q4 — Concrete struggle incident

**Ask**

* "Can you walk me through one concrete moment when students struggled with modelling in your course? I am interested in one real situation rather than a general list."

**Probe only if needed**

* "What was the task?"
* "What exactly were students stuck on?"
* "How did you recognise the problem?"
* "What conditions were shaping it: tool, time pressure, group work, unclear requirements, unfamiliar domain, transition to a new modelling family, or something else?"
* "What does that situation tell you about what students find hard in modelling?"

### Q5 — Starting barrier and dependence on examples

**Ask**

* "In your experience, is the main difficulty understanding the notation, or getting started independently when there is no teacher demonstration in front of them?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Do students wait for an example before they feel able to act?"
* "How do you decide when to demonstrate and when to step back?"
* "Do worked examples transfer well to new tasks, or do students still struggle once the surface details change?"

---

## 4. Support, quality, and participation (5–6 min)

### Q6 — What support actually helps

**Ask**

* "When students hit those difficulties, what support seems to help them most, and what tends not to help much?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Worked examples, templates, checklists, peer discussion, critique sessions, tool support?"
* "Do you respond differently to starting problems, semantic problems, and quality-judgement problems?"
* "Have you tried any support that sounded promising but failed in practice?"

### Q7 — Feedback, revision, and model quality criteria

**Ask**

* "How do students learn what counts as a good model in your course: through explicit criteria, through feedback on submissions, or mostly by inference?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Do you explicitly name criteria such as completeness, consistency, readability, rationale, or fit-for-purpose?"
* "Do students get chances to revise after feedback?"
* "Do your feedback practices help them self-critique later, or mainly correct the current assignment?"

### Q8 — Shy or passive students

**Ask**

* "What do shy or passive students do in your sessions — for example whiteboard work, labs, role-play, or stakeholder-style activities?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "What tends to fail for them?"
* "What do you do to bring them in without forcing public performance?"

---

## 5. Gap topics — use selectively (6–7 min)

### Q9 — Multi-family transitions

*(Use if the course covers more than one modelling family.)*

**Ask**

* "If you teach more than one modelling family, which transition do students fail most often?"
* "How do you teach consistency across models that represent different perspectives of the same problem?"

### Q10 — Tools and integration

**Ask**

* "Do your current tools mainly support modelling learning, or do they introduce extra friction?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Would you distinguish drawing tools from tools that actually teach or check modelling semantics?"
* "What support would matter most: semantic checking, guidance on next steps, templates, comparison, collaboration, execution?"
* "Have practical constraints such as cost, access, or usability shaped your tool choices?"
* "Are there gaps between family-specific tools and the integrated workflow you would like to teach?"

### Q11 — Scale and course conditions

**Ask**

* "Beyond tools, what conditions most shape modelling learning in your course: class size, workload, attendance, assignment domains, evaluation density, or something else?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "How does your pedagogy or feedback change in a large cohort versus a small class?"
* "How much does domain familiarity matter?"
* "How much do attendance and workload limit what students can actually learn?"

### Q12 — AI and assessment

**Ask**

* "Has artificial intelligence changed how you design assignments, weight exams versus projects, or handle plagiarism concerns?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Would you trust AI for semantic feedback on models, or worry it becomes an answer generator?"
* "What modelling competences should remain human-led — abstraction, stakeholder communication, justification?"
* "Has AI made you shift toward more exam-based or more guarded assessment?"

### Q13 — Requirements and socio-technical practice

*(Use for RE, goal-oriented, or stakeholder-heavy courses.)*

**Ask**

* "Do students work from vague briefs, interview stakeholders, or justify modelling choices in writing?"
* "Do you treat communication and empathy as part of modelling competence, not separate soft skills?"

---

## 6. Closing (2–3 min)

### Q14 — Most valuable realistic change

**Ask**

* "If you could make one realistic change that would most improve modelling learning in your context, what would it be?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Would that change target pedagogy, assessment, feedback, tools, or course conditions?"

### Q15 — Anything important missing

**Ask**

* "Is there anything important about teaching or learning modelling that I did not ask, but should have?"

**Close**

> Thank you. This was very helpful. If you are interested, I can share a short summary of findings later, and I may follow up with one or two clarifying questions.

---

## Semi-structured guidance

### Time triage (if running short)

**Always prioritise:** Q4, Q5, Q6, Q7, Q12.

**Context-specific priority:**

| Profile | Emphasise |
|---------|-----------|
| All lecturers (post-2024) | Q12 AI |
| Multi-family courses | Q9 |
| Large cohorts (100+ students) | Q11 |
| RE / requirements courses | Q13 |
| Tool-heavy courses | Q10 |

### Opportunistic follow-ups

* Why students wait for demonstrations
* How teachers decide scaffolding fade-out
* Whether students can self-check quality without prompting
* Why certain tools were abandoned
* How workload or absenteeism changes realistic expectations

### Interviewer discipline

* If the teacher gives a rich answer to Q4 or Q5, stay with it and cut later probes.
* Keep the tone exploratory: the goal is to let the teacher confirm, challenge, or nuance your interpretation.
* Do not try to ask every probe.
