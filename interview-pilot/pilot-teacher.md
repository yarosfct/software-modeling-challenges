# Teacher Interview Guide A - Comparable Revision (20-30 min)

This version keeps strong continuity with the current teacher pilot while removing low-yield questions, reducing overlap, and making the prompts more grounded. It is still semi-structured: ask every core question, use only the probes that help the interviewee become concrete, and follow interesting leads when they directly inform the thesis.

## 0. Opening (30-45s)

**What you say**

> Thanks for meeting with me. I am studying how modelling is taught and learned in higher education, with a focus on student challenges, the ways teachers respond to those challenges, and how context such as course design, feedback, tools, and institutional conditions shapes the experience.
> With your permission, I would like to record so I do not miss details. The interview should take around 20 to 30 minutes. You can skip any question.

*(If not recording: "I will take notes instead.")*

---

## 1. Teaching context (2-3 min) - [RQ3 context]

### Q1 - Teaching context and where modelling fits

**Ask**

* "Could you briefly describe your teaching context and where modelling fits into your courses?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Which courses include modelling, and at what level?"
* "Which modelling families or notations do you cover?"
* "Roughly how long have you been teaching modelling-related content?"

**Why this stays short**

* The goal here is only to situate the rest of the interview, not to spend time on descriptive details that do not help explain teaching or learning.

---

## 2. Learning goals, scaffolding, and assessment (5-6 min) - [RQ3, supports RQ1]

### Q2 - Intended competence and how it is assessed

**Ask**

* "When students finish your course, what should they be able to do with modelling, and how do you assess whether they can actually do it?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Which activities matter most in your course: interpreting, creating, refining, evaluating?"
* "What does minimum acceptable performance look like?"
* "Do your assessments capture what you most value, or is there any gap between what matters and what you can realistically grade?"

### Q3 - How you scaffold students from problem to model

**Ask**

* "How do you typically guide students from a problem statement to a first model and then to a better model?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Do you teach an explicit step-by-step approach?"
* "Where do students usually get stuck in that progression?"
* "Do you make model quality criteria explicit, or do students mostly learn them through feedback?"
* "Are there recurring misunderstandings that keep appearing even when students know the notation?"

**Interviewer note**

* If the teacher raises worked examples, demonstration, or gradual release, stay with that thread because it is highly relevant to your current analysis.

---

## 3. Observed student challenges (5-6 min) - [RQ1]

### Q4 - Main recurring modelling difficulties

**Ask**

* "From your perspective, where do students most often struggle when learning modelling?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Is the difficulty more about understanding meaning, deciding how to start, refining structure, or judging model quality?"
* "Do those difficulties change across notations or modelling families?"
* "Do they differ between beginners and more advanced students?"

### Q5 - Concrete incident walkthrough

**Ask**

* "Can you recall a specific moment when students struggled with modelling in your course? What was happening?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "What exactly were they stuck on?"
* "How did you notice the problem?"
* "What were the conditions: time pressure, tool, group work, unclear requirements, unfamiliar domain, or something else?"
* "If you redesigned that part of the course now, what would you change?"

**Interviewer note**

* This is one of the highest-yield questions. If the teacher gives a generic answer, ask for one concrete assignment, lab, or exam situation.

---

## 4. Support, feedback, and tools (6-7 min) - [RQ2, with RQ3 conditions]

### Q6 - What helps when students struggle

**Ask**

* "When students struggle with modelling, what do you typically do to help, and what seems to work best?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Do you respond differently depending on whether the problem is how to start, semantics, or quality judgement?"
* "Do worked examples, templates, checklists, peer review, or model critiques help?"
* "Have you tried anything that you expected to help but did not work well?"

### Q7 - Feedback and revision

**Ask**

* "How do you provide feedback on models, and how much opportunity do students have to revise?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Is feedback mostly one-shot grading or iterative?"
* "Do you give feedback on model quality criteria explicitly?"
* "Do revisions seem to improve students' ability to evaluate their own models, or mostly the current submission?"

### Q8 - Tool-related support and friction

**Ask**

* "What role do tools play in these challenges, and what kind of tool support would be most helpful in your teaching?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Do tools mainly help with checking, feedback, layout, collaboration, or something else?"
* "What tool friction do you see most: usability, setup overhead, cognitive load, or constraints that distort modelling?"
* "Have you changed tools, or avoided some tools, because of student experience?"

---

## 5. Contextual factors (3-4 min) - [RQ3]

### Q9 - Course conditions, domains, and constraints

**Ask**

* "What course or institutional conditions seem to shape how well students learn modelling in your context?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "How do workload and attendance affect modelling learning?"
* "How do you choose assignment domains or examples, and does domain familiarity matter?"
* "Do transitions between modelling families create extra difficulty?"

**Interviewer note**

* This question intentionally groups several shaping factors. Use only the probes that connect with what the teacher has already said.

---

## 6. Closing (2-3 min) - [RQ2 plus catch-all]

### Q10 - Most useful change

**Ask**

* "If you could change one or two things to improve modelling learning in your context, what would make the biggest difference?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Would that change be mainly about course design, feedback, tools, or workload conditions?"
* "Which change feels realistic rather than idealised?"

### Q11 - Anything missing

**Ask**

* "Is there anything important about teaching or learning modelling that I did not ask, but should have?"

**Close**

> Thank you. This was very helpful. If you are interested, I can share a short summary of findings later, and I may follow up with one or two clarifying questions.

---

## Quick RQ mapping

* **RQ1 (Challenges)** -> Q4, Q5
* **RQ2 (Strategies and support needs)** -> Q6, Q7, Q8, Q10
* **RQ3 (Socio-technical and pedagogical shaping factors)** -> Q1, Q2, Q3, Q5, Q8, Q9

---

## Semi-structured guidance

* Prioritise Q3, Q5, Q6, and Q7 if time becomes tight.
* Use follow-up probes only when the teacher stays abstract or mentions something directly relevant to the thesis.
* Good follow-up topics to explore if they arise naturally: starting a model, worked examples, transition between modelling families, model quality criteria, tool guidance, workload, and attendance.
* Avoid trying to cover every probe. The goal is depth on the most diagnostic issues, not full coverage of all prompts.
