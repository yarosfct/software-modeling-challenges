# Teacher Interview Pilot Guides (A / B / C)

This file holds all teacher interview variants for the software-modelling education study. Use the selection notes below to choose a variant, then run that section. Shared consent language and generic probes apply to every variant.

---

## 1. Purpose and when to use Variant A / B / C

Use this section to decide which teacher guide to take into the next pilot interview.

### Guide A — Comparable Revision

Choose this version when:

* you want continuity with the current pilot structure
* you want cleaner RQ traceability during analysis
* you expect the interviewee to be reflective and willing to answer broader course-design questions

Main characteristics:

* recognisable section flow
* less overlap than the previous version
* still broad enough to support cross-case comparison
* safer if you want to compare directly with earlier pilot data

### Guide B — Focused High-Yield Version

Choose this version when:

* you want richer evidence in a limited time window
* you want to prioritise concrete incidents over general descriptions
* you want to test the strongest hypotheses emerging from current lecturer coding and memos

Main characteristics:

* more direct and more diagnostic
* less time spent on generic background
* stronger focus on starting barriers, worked examples, quality judgement, transition between modelling families, tools, and workload constraints
* better if the goal is to refine the thesis argument quickly

### Guide C — Gap-Filling Version (current recommendation for new interviews)

Choose this version when:

* you are conducting interviews after the first coding cycle (S1-S3, L1-L4)
* you want to fill gaps: AI and assessment, scale-dependent pedagogy, shy students, multi-family transitions, integrated tooling, RE/socio-technical practice

Main characteristics:

* extends Guide B with Q8-Q13 gap blocks (use selectively)
* adds explicit probes on AI, class size, and passive students
* still incident-first with time triage

### Practical recommendation

For **new** interviews after the first analysis cycle, use **Guide C**. Use Guide B for shorter diagnostic sessions. Use Guide A for continuity with the earliest pilot structure.

### Shared interviewer reminders

* Do not try to ask every probe.
* Ask for one concrete incident whenever the teacher becomes too abstract.
* If a strong theme emerges early, stay with it and trim later sections.
* Semi-structured does not mean unfocused: follow interesting topics only when they clearly help answer the thesis questions.

## 2. Shared consent, intro, and probes

### Goals

#### What do I want to understand?

- How people experience software/conceptual modelling (UML, BPMN, iStar, ER, etc)
- What they find hard / easy / valuable / pointless.
- How they think about learning outcomes / competencies
- Which teaching strategies, tools, domains, assignments help or hinder.
- Ideas / requirements for better support

#### Style

- Semi-structured: I should have a backbone of questions, but it is better to follow interesting threads.
- Open and non-judgmental: no right/wrong answers.
- Grounded-theory-friendly:
  - Start broad (like "tell me about ...")
  - Use probing ("can you give me an example?")
  - Stay as close as possible to the participants own words.

### Generic intro for both groups — 2/3 min

- Thank the participant for taking their time to do the interview.
- Give some context about the interview background, like, "im working on a thesis about ...,"
- Explain why you picked that person for the interview : "im interested in your experience with ..., what works, what doesnt, what would help with ..."
- Then inform the participant about the expected/approcimated duration of the interview, inform that you will be taking notes or even recording audio, therefore getting the interview consent is priority ("if you are okay with, ill be ...").
- Assure the interviewer that his/her identity will remain anonymised, " i wont use your name or anything that directly identifies you in the thesis".
- Reasure the interviewer that there are no right or wrong answer and what im really looking for is honest opinions, that the interviewer is allowed and encouraged to make questions if anything is unclear, and if by chance there is some question that the interviewer doesnt want to answer, he can just say no.
- Lastly ask the interviewer if he got any questions before starting, and after that, ask permission for recording and start the interview.

### Follow-up prompts that can be useful

Whenever someone says something intersting, this can be used as generic probes:

- "Can you tell me a bit more about that?"
- "What do you mean by [their word] in this context?"
- "Can you give me a concrete example?"
- "How did you feel in that situation?"
- "What made that particularly easy/hard/frustrating...?"
- "How did you deal with that at the time?"
- "If you could change something about that, what would it be?"
- "Has this happened more than once? When else?"
- ... I'll add more later

## 3. Variant A — Comparable Revision (20-30 min)

This version keeps strong continuity with the current teacher pilot while removing low-yield questions, reducing overlap, and making the prompts more grounded. It is still semi-structured: ask every core question, use only the probes that help the interviewee become concrete, and follow interesting leads when they directly inform the thesis.

### 0. Opening (30-45s)

**What you say**

> Thanks for meeting with me. I am studying how modelling is taught and learned in higher education, with a focus on student challenges, the ways teachers respond to those challenges, and how context such as course design, feedback, tools, and institutional conditions shapes the experience.
> With your permission, I would like to record so I do not miss details. The interview should take around 20 to 30 minutes. You can skip any question.

*(If not recording: "I will take notes instead.")*

---

### 1. Teaching context (2-3 min) - [RQ3 context]

#### Q1 - Teaching context and where modelling fits

**Ask**

* "Could you briefly describe your teaching context and where modelling fits into your courses?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Which courses include modelling, and at what level?"
* "Which modelling families or notations do you cover?"
* "Roughly how long have you been teaching modelling-related content?"

**Why this stays short**

* The goal here is only to situate the rest of the interview, not to spend time on descriptive details that do not help explain teaching or learning.

---

### 2. Learning goals, scaffolding, and assessment (5-6 min) - [RQ3, supports RQ1]

#### Q2 - Intended competence and how it is assessed

**Ask**

* "When students finish your course, what should they be able to do with modelling, and how do you assess whether they can actually do it?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Which activities matter most in your course: interpreting, creating, refining, evaluating?"
* "What does minimum acceptable performance look like?"
* "Do your assessments capture what you most value, or is there any gap between what matters and what you can realistically grade?"

#### Q3 - How you scaffold students from problem to model

**Ask**

* "How do you typically guide students from a problem statement to a first model and then to a better model?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Do you teach an explicit step-by-step approach?"
* "Where do students usually get stuck in that progression?"
* "Do you make model quality criteria explicit, or do students mostly learn them through feedback?"
* "Are there recurring misunderstandings that keep appearing even when students know the notation?"

**Interviewer note**

* If the teacher raises worked examples, demonstration, or gradual release, stay with that thread because it is highly relevant to your current analysis.

---

### 3. Observed student challenges (5-6 min) - [RQ1]

#### Q4 - Main recurring modelling difficulties

**Ask**

* "From your perspective, where do students most often struggle when learning modelling?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Is the difficulty more about understanding meaning, deciding how to start, refining structure, or judging model quality?"
* "Do those difficulties change across notations or modelling families?"
* "Do they differ between beginners and more advanced students?"

#### Q5 - Concrete incident walkthrough

**Ask**

* "Can you recall a specific moment when students struggled with modelling in your course? What was happening?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "What exactly were they stuck on?"
* "How did you notice the problem?"
* "What were the conditions: time pressure, tool, group work, unclear requirements, unfamiliar domain, or something else?"
* "If you redesigned that part of the course now, what would you change?"

**Interviewer note**

* This is one of the highest-yield questions. If the teacher gives a generic answer, ask for one concrete assignment, lab, or exam situation.

---

### 4. Support, feedback, and tools (6-7 min) - [RQ2, with RQ3 conditions]

#### Q6 - What helps when students struggle

**Ask**

* "When students struggle with modelling, what do you typically do to help, and what seems to work best?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Do you respond differently depending on whether the problem is how to start, semantics, or quality judgement?"
* "Do worked examples, templates, checklists, peer review, or model critiques help?"
* "Have you tried anything that you expected to help but did not work well?"

#### Q7 - Feedback and revision

**Ask**

* "How do you provide feedback on models, and how much opportunity do students have to revise?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Is feedback mostly one-shot grading or iterative?"
* "Do you give feedback on model quality criteria explicitly?"
* "Do revisions seem to improve students' ability to evaluate their own models, or mostly the current submission?"

#### Q8 - Tool-related support and friction

**Ask**

* "What role do tools play in these challenges, and what kind of tool support would be most helpful in your teaching?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Do tools mainly help with checking, feedback, layout, collaboration, or something else?"
* "What tool friction do you see most: usability, setup overhead, cognitive load, or constraints that distort modelling?"
* "Have you changed tools, or avoided some tools, because of student experience?"

---

### 5. Contextual factors (3-4 min) - [RQ3]

#### Q9 - Course conditions, domains, and constraints

**Ask**

* "What course or institutional conditions seem to shape how well students learn modelling in your context?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "How do workload and attendance affect modelling learning?"
* "How do you choose assignment domains or examples, and does domain familiarity matter?"
* "Do transitions between modelling families create extra difficulty?"

**Interviewer note**

* This question intentionally groups several shaping factors. Use only the probes that connect with what the teacher has already said.

---

### 6. Closing (2-3 min) - [RQ2 plus catch-all]

#### Q10 - Most useful change

**Ask**

* "If you could change one or two things to improve modelling learning in your context, what would make the biggest difference?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Would that change be mainly about course design, feedback, tools, or workload conditions?"
* "Which change feels realistic rather than idealised?"

#### Q11 - Anything missing

**Ask**

* "Is there anything important about teaching or learning modelling that I did not ask, but should have?"

**Close**

> Thank you. This was very helpful. If you are interested, I can share a short summary of findings later, and I may follow up with one or two clarifying questions.

---

### Quick RQ mapping

* **RQ1 (Challenges)** -> Q4, Q5
* **RQ2 (Strategies and support needs)** -> Q6, Q7, Q8, Q10
* **RQ3 (Socio-technical and pedagogical shaping factors)** -> Q1, Q2, Q3, Q5, Q8, Q9

---

### Semi-structured guidance

* Prioritise Q3, Q5, Q6, and Q7 if time becomes tight.
* Use follow-up probes only when the teacher stays abstract or mentions something directly relevant to the thesis.
* Good follow-up topics to explore if they arise naturally: starting a model, worked examples, transition between modelling families, model quality criteria, tool guidance, workload, and attendance.
* Avoid trying to cover every probe. The goal is depth on the most diagnostic issues, not full coverage of all prompts.

## 4. Variant B — Focused High-Yield Version (20-30 min)

This version is designed to maximise thesis-relevant evidence in a short semi-structured interview. It is more direct, more incident-based, and less descriptive. It is the better choice when you want richer data rather than strong comparability with the earlier pilot.

### 0. Opening (30-45s)

**What you say**

> Thanks for meeting with me. I am studying how modelling is taught and learned in higher education, especially the difficulties students face, the kinds of support that help, and how teaching choices, tools, and course conditions shape that experience.
> With your permission, I would like to record so I do not miss details. This should take around 20 to 30 minutes. You can skip any question.

*(If not recording: "I will take notes instead.")*

---

### 1. Fast setup (1-2 min)

#### Q1 - Minimal teaching context

**Ask**

* "To situate your answers, could you briefly tell me what modelling-related course or courses you teach, and which modelling families you cover?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Introductory or advanced?"
* "How long have you been teaching this content?"

---

### 2. What students should learn, and where the course puts the pressure (4-5 min)

#### Q2 - Intended competence and assessment alignment

**Ask**

* "By the end of your course, what should students be able to do with modelling, and where in the course do you actually see whether they can do it?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Which activities matter most: interpreting, creating, refining, evaluating?"
* "What does a merely acceptable model look like, compared with a strong one?"
* "Is there anything you care about in modelling that your current assessment does not capture well?"

#### Q3 - How students are expected to get from problem to model

**Ask**

* "When students face a new modelling task, what process do you expect them to follow from the problem statement to a first workable model?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Do you teach that process explicitly?"
* "Where do they most often break down: getting started, abstraction, semantics, refinement, or quality checking?"
* "Are there misunderstandings that recur across different notations?"

---

### 3. Core evidence: concrete struggle and teacher interpretation (6-7 min)

#### Q4 - Concrete struggle incident

**Ask**

* "Can you walk me through one concrete moment when students struggled with modelling in your course? I am interested in one real situation rather than a general list."

**Probe only if needed**

* "What was the task?"
* "What exactly were students stuck on?"
* "How did you recognise the problem?"
* "What conditions were shaping it: tool, time pressure, group work, unclear requirements, unfamiliar domain, or transition to a new modelling family?"
* "What does that situation tell you about what students find hard in modelling?"

#### Q5 - Starting barrier and dependence on examples

**Ask**

* "In your experience, is the main difficulty understanding the notation, or getting started independently when there is no teacher demonstration in front of them?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Do students wait for an example before they feel able to act?"
* "How do you decide when to demonstrate and when to step back?"
* "Do worked examples transfer well to new tasks, or do students still struggle once the surface details change?"

**Interviewer note**

* This question is intentionally sharper than in the comparable version because it tests a central hypothesis from the lecturer data.

---

### 4. Support, feedback, and quality judgement (6-7 min)

#### Q6 - What support actually helps

**Ask**

* "When students hit those difficulties, what support seems to help them most, and what tends not to help much?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Worked examples, templates, checklists, peer discussion, critique sessions, tool support?"
* "Do you respond differently to starting problems, semantic problems, and quality-judgement problems?"
* "Have you tried any support that sounded promising but failed in practice?"

#### Q7 - Feedback, revision, and model quality criteria

**Ask**

* "How do students learn what counts as a good model in your course: through explicit criteria, through feedback on submissions, or mostly by inference?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Do you explicitly name criteria such as completeness, consistency, readability, or fit-for-purpose?"
* "Do students get chances to revise after feedback?"
* "Do your feedback practices help them self-critique later, or mainly correct the current assignment?"

---

### 5. Tools, transitions, and contextual constraints (4-5 min)

#### Q8 - Tools as help or friction

**Ask**

* "Do your current tools mainly support modelling learning, or do they introduce extra friction?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Would you distinguish drawing tools from tools that actually teach or check modelling?"
* "What support would matter most: semantic checking, guidance on next steps, templates, comparison, collaboration?"
* "Have practical constraints such as cost, access, or usability shaped your tool choices?"

#### Q9 - Course conditions that shape modelling learning

**Ask**

* "Beyond tools, what conditions most shape modelling learning in your course: workload, attendance, assignment domains, transitions between modelling families, something else?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "How much does domain familiarity matter?"
* "Do students struggle when moving from one modelling family to another?"
* "How much do attendance and workload limit what students can actually learn?"

---

### 6. Closing (2-3 min)

#### Q10 - Most valuable realistic change

**Ask**

* "If you could make one realistic change that would most improve modelling learning in your context, what would it be?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Would that change target pedagogy, assessment, feedback, tools, or course conditions?"

#### Q11 - Anything important missing

**Ask**

* "Is there anything important about teaching or learning modelling that I did not ask, but should have?"

**Close**

> Thank you. This was very helpful. If you are interested, I can share a short summary of findings later, and I may follow up with one or two clarifying questions.

---

### Why this version is different

* It reduces descriptive setup and moves faster to observable teaching practice.
* It privileges concrete incidents over general opinions.
* It directly tests themes that emerged from your current lecturer analysis: the starting barrier, dependence on worked examples, implicit quality criteria, transition between modelling families, tool support, and contextual constraints.

---

### Semi-structured guidance

* If the teacher gives a rich answer to Q4 or Q5, stay with it and cut later probes.
* Prioritise Q4, Q5, Q6, Q7, and Q9 if time becomes tight.
* Good opportunistic follow-up topics: why students wait for demonstrations, how teachers decide scaffolding fade-out, whether students can self-check quality, why certain tools were abandoned, and how workload or absenteeism changes what teachers can realistically expect.
* Keep the tone exploratory. The goal is not to confirm your current interpretation, but to let the teacher confirm, challenge, or nuance it.

## 5. Variant C — Gap-Filling Version (25–30 min)

This version extends Guide B (Variant B above) with probes for themes under-represented in the current lecturer corpus: multi-family transitions, scale-dependent pedagogy, shy/passive students, AI and assessment change, integrated tooling, and requirements/socio-technical teaching.

**Use when:** you want interviews that strengthen cross-case claims on RQ2/RQ3, especially AI, scale, collaboration, and RE-oriented practice.

---

### 0. Opening (30–45s)

*(If not recording: "I will take notes instead.")*

### 5. Gap topics — use selectively (6–7 min)

#### Q9 — Multi-family transitions

*(Use if the course covers more than one modelling family.)*

**Ask**

* "If you teach more than one modelling family, which transition do students fail most often?"
* "How do you teach consistency across models that represent different perspectives of the same problem?"

#### Q10 — Tools and integration

**Ask**

* "Do your current tools mainly support modelling learning, or do they introduce extra friction?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Would you distinguish drawing tools from tools that actually teach or check modelling semantics?"
* "What support would matter most: semantic checking, guidance on next steps, templates, comparison, collaboration, execution?"
* "Have practical constraints such as cost, access, or usability shaped your tool choices?"
* "Are there gaps between family-specific tools and the integrated workflow you would like to teach?"

#### Q11 — Scale and course conditions

**Ask**

* "Beyond tools, what conditions most shape modelling learning in your course: class size, workload, attendance, assignment domains, evaluation density, or something else?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "How does your pedagogy or feedback change in a large cohort versus a small class?"
* "How much does domain familiarity matter?"
* "How much do attendance and workload limit what students can actually learn?"

#### Q12 — AI and assessment

**Ask**

* "Has artificial intelligence changed how you design assignments, weight exams versus projects, or handle plagiarism concerns?"

**Probe only if needed**

* "Would you trust AI for semantic feedback on models, or worry it becomes an answer generator?"
* "What modelling competences should remain human-led — abstraction, stakeholder communication, justification?"
* "Has AI made you shift toward more exam-based or more guarded assessment?"

#### Q13 — Requirements and socio-technical practice

*(Use for RE, goal-oriented, or stakeholder-heavy courses.)*

**Ask**

* "Do students work from vague briefs, interview stakeholders, or justify modelling choices in writing?"
* "Do you treat communication and empathy as part of modelling competence, not separate soft skills?"

### Semi-structured guidance

#### Time triage (if running short)

**Always prioritise:** Q4, Q5, Q6, Q7, Q12.

**Context-specific priority:**

| Profile | Emphasise |
|---------|-----------|
| All lecturers (post-2024) | Q12 AI |
| Multi-family courses | Q9 |
| Large cohorts (100+ students) | Q11 |
| RE / requirements courses | Q13 |
| Tool-heavy courses | Q10 |

#### Opportunistic follow-ups

* Why students wait for demonstrations
* How teachers decide scaffolding fade-out
* Whether students can self-check quality without prompting
* Why certain tools were abandoned
* How workload or absenteeism changes realistic expectations

#### Interviewer discipline

* If the teacher gives a rich answer to Q4 or Q5, stay with it and cut later probes.
* Keep the tone exploratory: the goal is to let the teacher confirm, challenge, or nuance your interpretation.
* Do not try to ask every probe.
