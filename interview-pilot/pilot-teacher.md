# Teacher Interview Plan (20-30 min)

## 1. Structure and timing (rough)

1. Background and teaching context — 4-5 min
2. Learning outcomes and course desing - 7-8 min
3. Observed student challenges - 6-7 min
4. Tools, assignments, motivation & inclusion — 6-7 min
5. Support needs and closing — 3-4 min


## 2. Background and teaching context (4-5 min)

**Q1**. Could you tell me abit about your background and how modeling fits into your teaching?

PP:
- Which courses do you teach that involve modeling?
- Which notations or methods do you cover? (e.g., UML, BPMN, iStar, ER, domain-specific...)
- Approximately how many semesters/years have you taught these topics?


## 3. Learning outcomes and course design (7-8 min)

**Q2**. When you design or revise a modeling-related course, what are the main learning goals you have in mind for students?

PP:
- When/If a student finishes your course, what should they definitely be able to do?
- Do you distinguish between basic and advanced goals? how?
- Do you explicitly think in terms of cognitive levels (e.g., understanding/applying/analyzing/evaluating), or more in terms of content coverage?


**Q3**. How do you typically scaffold modeling in your courses?

PP:
- How do you introduce a new notation? (lectures, small examples, exercises...)
- Is there a progression, for instance from simple models / symbols to more complex ones?
- Do you explicitly teach strategies for going from text to model, or for refining and checking models?
- (If interesting) Have you ever mapped your course content to frameworks like Bloom or something similar? What did you find?


**Q4**. In terms of assessment, what kinds of tasks or exams do you use to evaluate modeling skills?

PP:
- Do you mostly ask students to create models, interpret models, critique models, or a mix?
- Do you assess process (how they work) or mainly the final models?
- Do you include any elements that target evaluation or reflection (e.g., comparing alternatives, explaining decisions)?

## 4. Observed student challenges (6-7 min)

**Q5**. From your perspective, what do students usually find most difficult about learning modeling?

PP:
- Is it more about conceptual understanding, applying to real problems, domain abstraction, or judging model quality?
- Do you see recurring misconceptions or mistakes? (e.g., confusing certain elements, typical quality problems...)
- Do these difficulties change between introductory and advanced courses, or between different notations?


**Q6**. Can you recall a situation where students were struggling with modeling in your course? What was happening?

PP:
- What did they struggle with exactly?
- How did you notice? (questions, poor mid-term/exam results, complaints...)
- How did you respond at the time?
- Looking back, would you respond differently now?

## 5. Tools, assignments, motivation and inclusion (6-7 min)

**Q7**. What tools do you use or recommend for modeling in your courses, and how do you feel about them in an educational context?

PP:
- Why did you choose these tools?
- Have you noticed if those tools help or hinder students learning?
- Do you see a difference between tools meant for industrial use and tools suitable for teaching?
- Have you changed tools in the past because of student feedback or your own experience?

**Q8**. How do you choose the domains or examples for modeling assignments?

PP:
- What factors influence your choice? (relevance to programme, your expertise, perceived student interest...)
- Do you consider aspects like motivation or inclusion/diversity when selecting domains?
- Have you ever experimented with letting students choose their own domain? How did that go?
- (Optional) Have you noticed if certain domains seem to resonate more with some groups of students than others?

**Q9**. How do you usually provide feedback on students models or modeling process?

PP:
- Is feedback mainly written comments, grades, in-class discussions, automated checks, peer review...?
- Do students have multiple opportunities to revise their models based on feedback, or is it mostly one-shot?
- Have students ever commented on your feedback practices (positive or negative)?

## 6. Support needs, ideas and closing (3-4 min)

**Q10**. If you imagine an "ideal" learning environment or tool to support modeling in your course, what feature or supports would it have?

PP:
- Anything regarding explanations / guided examples?
- Anything regarding automated checks / hints?
- Anything regarding visualisation, layout, multiple views, templates?
- What would make your teaching job easier without oversimplifying the subject?

**Q11**. Looking at the next few years, what would you personally like to change or improve in how you teach modeling?

PP:
- Is there something you feel is missing in current curricula or materials?
- Are there any research results, tools or communities you wish you had easier access to?


**Q12**. Is there anything else about teaching / learning modeling that you think I should have asked, but didnt?

Thank the teacher, offer to share a summary of my results if they are interested and close.
