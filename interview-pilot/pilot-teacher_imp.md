# Teacher Interview Script (20–30 min)

## 0) Opening (30–45s)

**What you say**

> Thanks for meeting with me. I’m studying how modelling is taught and learned in higher education, focusing on challenges, coping strategies/support needs, and how context (tools, course design, domains, feedback) shapes the experience.
> With your permission, I’d like to record so I don’t miss details. The interview should take about 20–30 minutes. You can skip any question.

*(If not recording: “I’ll take notes instead.”)*

---

## 1) Teaching context (3–4 min) — **(Context for RQ3; also frames RQ1/RQ2)**

### Q1 — Teaching background and where modelling fits **[RQ3-context]**

**Ask**

* “Could you briefly describe your teaching context and where modelling fits into your courses?”

**Probes (use only if needed)**

* “Which courses include modelling, and at what level (intro/advanced)?”
* “Which modelling families do you cover (e.g., UML, BPMN, ER, iStar/goal modelling, MDE)?”
* “Roughly how many semesters/years have you taught modelling-related content?”
* “Typical class size / team projects or individual work?”

**Transition**

> Thanks — now I’d like to understand what you aim students to learn, and how your course design supports that.

---

## 2) Learning goals and course design (6–7 min) — **(Links strongly to RQ3 and sets up RQ1)**

### Q2 — Intended modelling competence **[RQ3 → enables RQ1]**

**Ask**

* “When a student finishes your course, what should they be able to do with modelling?”

**Probes**

* “Which modelling activities matter most in your course: interpreting, creating, refining, evaluating?”
* “Do you distinguish ‘minimum expected’ vs ‘excellent’ performance? What’s the difference?”
* “What does *good enough* look like for you?”

### Q3 — How you scaffold the modelling work **[RQ3]**

**Ask**

* “How do you typically scaffold students from the problem statement to a model and a better model?”

**Probes**

* “Do you teach an explicit step-by-step approach (e.g., identify concepts/actors first, then relations, then refine)?”
* “Where do students usually get stuck in that progression?”
* “Do you teach model checking / quality criteria explicitly, or mostly implicitly through feedback?”
* “Do you include reflection/comparison tasks (e.g., justify choices, compare alternatives)?”

### Q4 — Assessment focus across modelling activities **[RQ3 → aligns with RQ1]**

**Ask**

* “How do you assess modelling competence in your course?”

**Probes**

* “Do assessments mostly test final models, or also process and reasoning?”
* “Which activities are assessed most: interpret, create, refine, evaluate?”
* “Do you explicitly grade quality dimensions (completeness, consistency, readability, fit-for-purpose)?”

**Transition**

> Great — now I want to focus on where students struggle and how those struggles show up in practice.

---

## 3) Observed student challenges (6–8 min) — **(Core RQ1)**

### Q5 — Biggest recurring challenges **[RQ1]**

**Ask**

* “From your perspective, what are the most common challenges students face in modelling?”

**Probes (choose 1–2 max)**

* “Are the challenges more **conceptual** (meaning/semantics) or more **procedural** (how to build/refine)?”
* “Do you see issues mainly in: interpreting requirements, deciding what to include/omit, refining structure, or judging quality?”
* “Do challenges differ by notation/model family (e.g., UML vs BPMN vs goal modelling)?”
* “Do challenges differ between beginners and advanced students?”

### Q6 — Concrete incident walkthrough (high value) **[RQ1 + RQ3]**

**Ask**

* “Can you recall a specific moment when students struggled with modelling in your course? What was happening?”

**Probes**

* “What exactly were they stuck on? (e.g., abstraction decisions, semantics, consistency, quality)”
* “How did you notice it? (questions, submissions, labs, exams)”
* “What were the conditions? (time pressure, tool, group work, unclear requirements, domain unfamiliarity)”
* “If you could redesign that part of the course, what would you change?”

**Transition**

> Thanks — now I’d like to explore how you and students respond to these challenges, and what kinds of support seem to help.

---

## 4) Coping strategies and support needs (6–7 min) — **(Core RQ2, with RQ3 causes/conditions)**

### Q7 — What you do when students struggle **[RQ2]**

**Ask**

* “When students struggle with modelling, what do you typically do to help, and what seems to work best?”

**Probes**

* “Do you use worked examples, templates, checklists, peer review, or model critiques?”
* “Do you intervene differently depending on the type of difficulty (semantics vs 'how to start' vs quality judgement)?”
* “What do students themselves do that you consider effective (or ineffective)?”

### Q8 — Tool-related supports and constraints **[RQ2 + RQ3]**

**Ask**

* “What role do tools play in these challenges — and what tool support would be most helpful in your teaching?”

**Probes**

* “Do tools mostly help via syntax checking, feedback/hints, layout, model views, versioning, or collaboration?”
* “What tool friction do you see: usability, setup overhead, cognitive load, constraints that distort modelling?”
* “Have you changed tools (or avoided tools) because of student experience?”

### Q9 — Feedback and iteration (key lever) **[RQ2 + RQ3]**

**Ask**

* “How do you provide feedback on models, and how much opportunity do students have to revise?”

**Probes**

* “Is feedback mostly one-shot grading, or iterative cycles?”
* “Do you give feedback on *model quality criteria* explicitly?”
* “Any feedback approaches that improved students’ ability to evaluate/refine models?”

**Transition**

> Finally, I’d like to ask about contextual factors — domains, course structure — and then close with your ideal supports.

---

## 5) Socio-technical shaping factors (3–4 min) — **(Core RQ3)**

### Q10 — Domains and authenticity **[RQ3]**

**Ask**

* “How do you choose assignment domains/examples, and how do those choices affect student engagement or performance?”

**Probes**

* “Do you see differences when domains are familiar vs unfamiliar?”
* “Do you allow students to choose domains? What happened when you did?”
* “Any domain choices that unintentionally excluded or demotivated some students?”

*(Keep this short; you can also drop the inclusion probe if time is tight.)*

---

## 6) Closing and “ideal support” (2–3 min) — **(RQ2 + implications)**

### Q11 — Ideal supports / recommendations **[RQ2]**

**Ask**

* “If you could change one or two things to improve modelling learning in your context, what would you change?”

**Probes**

* “Course design (scaffolding, sequencing, assessment)?”
* “Feedback mechanisms?”
* “Tool features or infrastructure?”
* “Any small change that would have a big impact?”

### Q12 — Anything missing **[RQ1/RQ2/RQ3 catch-all]**

**Ask**

* “Is there anything important about teaching or learning modelling that I didn’t ask, but should have?”

**Close**

> Thank you — this was very helpful. If you’re interested, I can share a short summary of findings later, and I may follow up with one or two clarifying questions.

---

# Quick mapping (so your interview stays “RQ-driven”)

* **RQ1 (Challenges)** → Q5, Q6 (plus parts of Q4)
* **RQ2 (Strategies + support needs)** → Q7, Q8, Q9, Q11
* **RQ3 (Socio-technical shaping factors)** → Q1–Q4 (design/assessment), Q6 (conditions), Q8–Q10 (tools/domains/feedback)

---

## Small optional upgrade (high impact, 2–3 min)

If you’re doing **activity/artefact-centered elicitation with teachers**, insert this right after Q6:

**Mini-elicitation prompt** **[RQ1+RQ2+RQ3]**

* “Could you briefly describe one concrete modelling assignment/exam task you use, and what a strong vs weak submission looks like?”

  * Probe: “Which modelling activities does it test (interpret/create/refine/evaluate)?”
  * Probe: “What mistakes are most diagnostic?”
  * Probe: “What feedback/support would prevent those?”

This gives you very “grounded” data without needing them to open files live.
