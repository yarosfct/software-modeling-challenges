1. What is happening here?
Near the end of the interview, the lecturer steps back from the classroom and reflects on a broader problem: modelling is pedagogically valuable, but its wider use appears fragmented, inconsistent, and often incorrect.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This matters because it frames modelling education within a larger ecosystem of weak standardization and uncertain industry uptake. Students may be learning something whose external status is less stable than lecturers would like.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
There is a mismatch between the lecturer's belief in modelling as support for software development and the practical reality of fragmented notations, low consensus, and incorrect use in real settings.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "I still wonder why companies in industry do not use modeling artifacts more often"
- "It would be a good idea to have only one"
- "it is not easy to reach a consensus"
- "many people use it incorrectly"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Modelling is presented as useful for managing complexity, yet the lecturer also sees limited uptake and poor quality in broader practice.

7. Implications
The value proposition of modelling may need to be made more explicit and credible for students if they also perceive weak uptake outside the classroom.
