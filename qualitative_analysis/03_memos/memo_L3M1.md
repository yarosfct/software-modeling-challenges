TITLE: Synthesis from natural language is treated as core modelling competence
DATE: 09/04/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview 3
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: synthesis-centered modelling; natural-language-to-model translation; example-based interpretation before synthesis; mixed interpretation-and-synthesis exams
CATEGORY / THEME: Intended modelling competence

1. What is happening here?
The lecturer describes the main modelling competence as synthesis: students receive a natural-language problem description and are expected to produce a model from it. Interpretation still matters, but mainly as preparation for later independent creation.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This gives a very clear formulation of what modelling ability means in this course. The emphasis is not on naming symbols or recognizing finished diagrams, but on translating from informal problem descriptions into formal representations.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
The lecturer treats modelling as a constructive transformation activity. That means teaching must support interpretation, abstraction, and representation together rather than separately.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "the emphasis was more on synthesis, meaning creating models"
- "giving students a natural-language description and asking them to produce a model from it"
- "students first had to learn how to interpret example models"
- "we usually used a mixture of model interpretation and model synthesis"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Exams still combine interpretation and synthesis because full synthesis takes time. So the central competence is acknowledged, but not always fully assessable under time constraints.
