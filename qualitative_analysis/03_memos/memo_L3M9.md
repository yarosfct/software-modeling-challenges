TITLE: Decomposing messy real-world problems is a missing methodology
DATE: 09/04/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview 3
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: breaking problems into smaller parts; textbooks lack decomposition guidance; real-world decomposition exceeds textbook examples
CATEGORY / THEME: Teaching support / methodology

1. What is happening here?
The lecturer identifies a gap not only in tools but in modelling pedagogy itself: students need better guidance on how to break complex real-world problems into smaller parts that can be modelled, yet textbooks often do not teach that well.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This points to a methodological gap at the heart of modelling education. The difficulty is not only understanding notation; it is learning how to decompose large, messy situations into modelable structures.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Many pedagogical examples are too neat and too small. They may teach notation, but they do not adequately teach how to approach realistic complexity.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "break the problem into smaller parts"
- "what I find missing... is good guidance on how to break down a complex real-world problem"
- "Textbook examples fit on one page, but real problems do not"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The lecturer uses decomposition as a practical scaffolding move in the lab, yet also says the broader teaching literature and textbooks do not support that methodology well.
