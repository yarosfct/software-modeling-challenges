MEMO ID: S4M3
TITLE: Worked examples are useful when they expose modelling rationale
DATE: 09/06/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 4 (reconstructed)
RELATED RQ(s): RQ1, RQ2
RELATED CODES: worked examples teach reasoning not shapes; rationale for relation choice; calibration through contrasting models; need for complete worked examples before practice
CATEGORY / THEME: Worked examples and reasoning visibility

1. What is happening here?
S4 values examples when they explain why a modelling decision is made, especially choices around association vs composition and the boundary between too much and too little detail.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This supports the larger thesis claim that students need procedural and evaluative modelling knowledge, not just notation memorisation.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
The underlying issue is invisible reasoning. A final diagram shows an answer but not the chain of judgement that produced it.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "It helps most with the reasoning. It is not just about memorising shapes"
- "why one relation is composition and another is just association"
- "one too detailed and one too abstract"
- "complete worked examples ... with explanation of choices"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Examples can either scaffold reasoning or become templates for copying. The difference lies in whether the teacher explains alternatives and decision criteria.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
S3 also benefited from multiple points of contact and examples. Lecturer cases emphasise that students struggle to start and refine models; S4 shows that contrastive examples can teach refinement judgement.

7. Implications
Teaching materials should include full examples with commentary: initial interpretation, candidate elements, rejected alternatives, final model, and explanation of quality criteria.

8. Follow-up questions
Would students use complete examples before labs to participate more confidently? How can examples avoid becoming rigid answer patterns?
