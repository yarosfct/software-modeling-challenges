MEMO ID: S1M1
TITLE: Modelling is valued as communication, but only up to a point
DATE: 23/03/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 1
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: modelling as bridge between implementation and communication; readability for communication; usefulness has a limit; modelling as extra documentation; modelling clarifies system fit; modelling supports prioritization
CATEGORY / THEME: Meaning of modelling / usefulness

1. What is happening here?
The student describes modelling as a communication and abstraction device that helps clarify how a system fits together and supports planning. At the same time, he says that after a certain point it starts to feel arbitrary and like extra documentation.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This shows a mixed orientation to modelling. The student does not reject modelling outright, but its value appears conditional on whether it still supports understanding and coordination.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Modelling seems useful when it reduces complexity and supports action, but loses value when it becomes detached from immediate development needs or becomes too formalized.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "bridge the implementation and the sort of communication aspect of software engineering"
- "transmit the system concept in a way that is easily readable"
- "it sort of clarifies where everything fits in"
- "having a diagram definitely helps with... setting the priorities"
- "it feels a bit arbitrary and just like extra documentation"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The student sees modelling as important for communication, yet also as something that can become excessive documentation. The same practice is both enabling and burdensome depending on how far it goes.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
Compare with the lecturer interview, where modelling is clearly valued educationally. The student perspective adds that usefulness may drop when modelling no longer feels connected to concrete development work.

7. Implications
Teaching and tools may need to make the practical payoff of modelling more visible, especially for students who are not strongly invested in modelling as a discipline.

8. Follow-up questions
At what point does modelling begin to feel like "extra documentation" for students? Does this vary by notation, task, or domain?
