TITLE: Requirements modelling spans goal-oriented and object-oriented families
DATE: 08/06/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview 4
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: early and late requirements coverage; iStar for actor-goal modelling; goal refinements translated to UML; iStar and NFR Framework taught; informal-to-structured requirements translation; cross-model consistency requirement
CATEGORY / THEME: Curriculum / intended competence

1. What is happening here?
The lecturer teaches Requirements Engineering as a multi-family progression: iStar and the NFR Framework for early, goal-oriented requirements, then UML for later, more concrete specification. Students must move from organisational goals and stakeholders to structured models and eventually justify model choices and quality.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This is a strong example of modelling education that is not notation-centric. The course treats modelling as a chain of representations for different questions: why, who, what quality, and how the system is structured.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Students need to understand complementarity across paradigms, not just learn separate languages. The teaching intent is integration and consistency across perspectives.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "The course covers both early requirements and late requirements"
- "I teach iStar because it focuses on actors as active entities with their own goals"
- "Later, these refinements are translated into UML models"
- "I teach goal-oriented languages, including iStar and the NFR Framework"
- "Different models represent different perspectives of the same problem, so they must be consistent with each other"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The curricular vision is integrated, but later codes show tool support is fragmented across families. Teaching integration exceeds available tooling.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
Compared with lecturer interview 3, whose course centred on broad UML coverage and executable models, L4 is more requirements- and goal-oriented before object modelling. Compared with lecturer interview 1 and 2, the natural-language-to-model translation challenge appears again, but here across multiple modelling families rather than UML alone.

7. Implications
Modelling education at advanced levels may need explicit bridging activities between goal-oriented and object-oriented representations, plus consistency checks across them.

8. Follow-up questions
Which transition is hardest for students: iStar to NFR, or requirements models to UML? Do students themselves see these as one workflow or separate tasks?
