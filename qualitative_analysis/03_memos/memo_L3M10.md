TITLE: Tool requirements differ across modelling families, especially around collaboration and execution
DATE: 09/04/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview 3
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: BPMN requires collaboration-capable tooling; cooperative editing and traceability in one environment; Signavio as integrated feedback environment; missing execution or simulation in BPMN tools; BPMN richer than activity-diagram analogy; research-workflow examples motivate masters students
CATEGORY / THEME: Tooling / modelling-family differences

1. What is happening here?
When the lecturer turns to BPMN, the desired tool profile changes. Collaboration, traceability, and in-environment feedback become central, while execution and simulation remain desirable but are missing in the current tool.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This shows that the "right" modelling tool depends on the modelling family and course goals. Tool needs in UML are centered on typed validity and executability, while BPMN foregrounds collaborative process work.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Different modelling families support different practices, so one generic tool model may not fit all teaching contexts. BPMN especially seems tied to shared work and process visibility.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "one very important feature is the ability to collaborate on models"
- "observe the work remotely, trace changes, and provide feedback directly in the same environment"
- "it lets me provide feedback directly in the same environment where students are modeling"
- "What it lacks... is model execution or simulation"
- "BPMN is very rich and much more sophisticated"
- "that connection to research is often motivating"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Signavio is strong on collaboration and feedback, but weaker on execution. The earlier UML tool had stronger executability, but less built-in pedagogical guidance and collaboration.
