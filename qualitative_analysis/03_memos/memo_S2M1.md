MEMO ID: S2M1
TITLE: Modelling is valued as visual abstraction and explanation
DATE: 23/03/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 2
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: abstraction as first association; visual abstraction of complexity; modelling for outsider understanding; modelling useful for solo thinking; modelling relieves mental complexity; comparative diagrams explain design change; diagrams outperform verbal explanation
CATEGORY / THEME: Meaning of modelling / usefulness

1. What is happening here?
The student defines modelling primarily as abstraction. He sees it as a visual way to simplify complex systems, help outsiders understand them, and sometimes even help himself manage complexity.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This student has a very strong positive baseline view of modelling. Unlike a more skeptical position, he treats modelling as a useful explanatory device almost by definition.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Modelling is most valuable when it condenses complexity into something that can be grasped visually and compared, especially when words alone would be cumbersome.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "Abstraction absolutely"
- "It's a way to abstract complex systems and processes... visually"
- "people who maybe don't understand a lot of it can get a good grasp of it"
- "making a little diagram... can simplify it not only for you, but... also for others"
- "through words I wouldn't be able to explain as easily"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The student clearly values modelling, but still describes it as a means to an end rather than something central to how he works.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
Compared with student interview 1, this student is less ambivalent about the usefulness of modelling itself. The stronger tension here is not usefulness versus uselessness, but formal frameworks versus intuitive representation.

7. Implications
Teaching can build on students' intuitive sense that modelling is useful for explanation and complexity reduction, rather than introducing it first as a formal obligation.

8. Follow-up questions
Do other students also define modelling so strongly through abstraction? Is this view stronger when they have current workplace or dissertation experience?
