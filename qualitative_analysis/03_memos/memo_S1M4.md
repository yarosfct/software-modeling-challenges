TITLE: Readability anchors the student's sense of model quality
DATE: 23/03/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 1
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: readability for communication; selective omission for clarity; readability over exhaustiveness; self-evaluation by readability correctness and requirements; learning from other diagrams; balanced abstraction
CATEGORY / THEME: Quality criteria

1. What is happening here?
The student consistently judges models through readability, clarity, requirement fit, and an appropriate level of abstraction. He is willing to omit side effects or extra detail if they make the diagram more confusing.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This indicates that students may already hold practical quality criteria even when they cannot recall formal checklists or explicit quality instruction.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Quality is understood from a communicative perspective. A good model is not the one with the most detail, but the one that remains understandable and still captures the important parts.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "if it's something that is in the critical path... I absolutely will include it"
- "side effects... I would be okay with ignoring them"
- "readability... more than... 100% consistent with the real world"
- "I look for readability and correctness and like matching requirements"
- "just enough to make it clear and consistent"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The student prioritizes readability over exhaustiveness, which may sometimes conflict with formal completeness expectations in assessment.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
This is worth comparing with the lecturer's explicit grading dimensions of completeness, consistency, and readability. The student independently recalls readability and correctness, but not a formal evaluative scheme.

7. Implications
Quality support for students may be most effective if it builds on readability and communicability as entry points, then connects them to more formal modelling criteria.

8. Follow-up questions
Do other students also foreground readability? How do students decide when omission is acceptable versus problematic?
