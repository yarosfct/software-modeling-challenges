CATEGORY / THEME: Student model-quality criteria

1. What is happening here?
S4 has a personal model-quality checklist based on being able to explain elements, match requirements, and make the diagram readable for a supervisor.

2. Why is this analytically important?
The student independently converges on criteria similar to lecturer language: clarity, consistency, requirements fit, and communicative purpose.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Quality is learned through feedback and audience awareness rather than abstract rubric memorisation. The student operationalises quality by asking whether the model can be defended and understood.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "can I explain every element in plain language?"
- "does the diagram match the written requirements?"
- "readability matters... because my supervisor will review it"
- "I choose the one that is easier to explain in the report"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The student says criteria were mentioned in theory, but practical checklist development happened through feedback on drafts. Formal criteria alone did not become usable until applied.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
This aligns with S3's symbols-to-words check and L4's emphasis on meaning, rationale, and model quality. It strengthens the category of model quality as communicative and semantic fit.

7. Implications
Assessment rubrics should be translated into student-facing self-check questions, with examples of how to apply them to actual diagrams.

8. Follow-up questions
Which quality criteria do students naturally develop, and which remain dependent on explicit teaching?
