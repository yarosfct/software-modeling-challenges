TITLE: The student wants examples, templates, and incremental progression
DATE: 23/03/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 1
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: trial-and-error modelling from text; components-before-actors strategy; limited explicit procedural instruction; earlier iterative feedback would help; preference for example-based support; text-to-model examples as needed support; template-based support; desire for incremental modelling instruction; build complexity gradually; same evolving case for continuity
CATEGORY / THEME: Teaching support / scaffolding

1. What is happening here?
The student describes modelling from text as trial and error and has only a partial personal strategy for starting. He asks for examples, templates, earlier feedback, and an incremental teaching approach that begins with smaller systems before integrating more complexity.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This strongly supports the idea that students need procedural scaffolding, not just conceptual explanation. The student is effectively describing what kind of support would make modelling more learnable.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
The challenge appears to be proceduralizing the move from text to model while managing notation uncertainty. Without visible process support, the student falls back on guess-and-revise.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "I will make a model that is incorrect, and then... go back and forth"
- "it was more just like concepts. Here's the text, get to it"
- "examples for me personally are more useful"
- "this text description, translates to this model"
- "having templates... would be a great help"
- "more incremental approach to the diagrams"
- "really small system... then sorta try put everything together"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The student can articulate some personal heuristics, like starting with components, but still frames the overall modelling process as insufficiently supported.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
This aligns well with the lecturer's emphasis on worked examples and step-by-step guidance. The student perspective confirms that those forms of support are recognizable and desirable.

7. Implications
Pedagogy or tools should externalize the process of modelling from text through examples, staged tasks, templates, and iterative feedback loops.

8. Follow-up questions
Do these supports help students become independent, or do they risk over-structuring the activity? Which scaffold is most valuable first: examples, templates, or feedback timing?
