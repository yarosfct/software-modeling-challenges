CATEGORY / THEME: AI and tool-mediated feedback

1. What is happening here?
S5 uses ChatGPT more openly than peers to brainstorm alternative class diagrams and simplify sequence descriptions, but the strongest desired tool is not generative AI; it is semantic checking across diagrams.

2. Why is this analytically important?
The case indicates a shift in student tool practices while preserving the need for modelling understanding. AI is attractive for options and explanations, but students still value validation and consistency feedback.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
The underlying issue is feedback granularity. Students want tools to catch semantic mismatches they might miss, such as operations in sequence diagrams not existing in class diagrams.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "suggest alternative class diagrams from a bullet list"
- "simplify an overcomplicated sequence-diagram description"
- "I would not submit raw AI output"
- "this message does not match any operation in your class diagram"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
AI can support learning but may threaten assessment integrity. S5 manages this by treating AI output as a draft to compare and rewrite.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
S4 and S6 also frame AI as tutor/explainer rather than final model generator. L3 and L4 raise related concerns about AI disruption and semantic feedback.

7. Implications
Future tool support could combine LLM explanation with formal semantic checking and explicit traceability feedback.

8. Follow-up questions
How do students distinguish acceptable AI support from prohibited model generation? Do course policies shape this boundary?
