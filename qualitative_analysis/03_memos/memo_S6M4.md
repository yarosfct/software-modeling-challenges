TITLE: Role-play and stakeholder interviews teach requirements as perspective-dependent
DATE: 09/06/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 6 (reconstructed)
RELATED RQ(s): RQ1, RQ2, RQ3
RELATED CODES: vague authentic campus-service brief; stakeholder interviews diversify requirements; role-play reveals stakeholder-dependent requirements; model synthesises interview data before features; strategic model delays premature feature focus
CATEGORY / THEME: Authenticity, elicitation, and perspective-taking

1. What is happening here?
S6 describes a requirements course where students worked from a deliberately short brief, identified stakeholders, conducted role-play or optional real interviews, and produced divergent requirement lists.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This case shows how teaching design can make requirements discovery visible. Students learn that requirements are not simply extracted from a task sheet but negotiated through stakeholder perspectives.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
The underlying issue is moving from teacher-given specifications to stakeholder-dependent problem understanding. Modelling becomes a way to synthesise perspectives before implementation.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "deliberately short brief"
- "Different pairs produced different requirement lists after interviews"
- "requirements change depending on who you ask"
- "Without the strategic model, we would have jumped to features too early"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Role-play can feel artificial, but it still teaches a real phenomenon: requirements shift with stakeholders. Vague briefs are stressful but authentic.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
L4 uses stakeholder interviews and intentionally vague descriptions. S6 provides evidence that students may initially resist but later understand the purpose.

7. Implications
Even simulated stakeholder interaction can be valuable if it is linked to explicit modelling tasks and reflection on how requirements changed.

8. Follow-up questions
How realistic must role-play be to support learning? Does optional real stakeholder contact improve commitment or model quality?
