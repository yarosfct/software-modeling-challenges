MEMO ID: L3M5
TITLE: Labs are support spaces, not substitutes for missed theory
DATE: 09/04/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview 3
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: high no-show rate; large-scale modelling course; labs should not reteach theory; written lab guides and slide signposting
CATEGORY / THEME: Contextual factors / course design

1. What is happening here?
The lecturer explicitly rejects the idea that lab sessions should function as replacement lectures for students who skipped theory classes. In a large course with many no-shows, labs are meant to facilitate learning and remove barriers, not repeat the whole conceptual foundation.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This highlights an important institutional condition for modelling learning. Teaching support is designed with assumptions about attendance and prior preparation, and those assumptions can break down when many students disengage from theory sessions.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
The lecturer sees a division of pedagogical labor: theory introduces and frames the concepts, while labs support application. If students miss the first part, the second cannot fully compensate at scale.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "I had around 20 to 30 percent of students who were essentially no-shows"
- "we had between 200 and 300 students in total"
- "the lab should not become a repeated theory session"
- "A lab assistant should be there to facilitate learning and remove barriers"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Students may need recovery support when they miss theory, but the course structure cannot absorb repeated individualized reteaching for a large cohort.

7. Implications
Any modelling intervention has to account for how students move between theory, guided practice, and self-study, especially in large classes.
