TITLE: Quality is assessed explicitly, but self-checking is less explicit
DATE: 19/03/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: no explicit model checking instruction; explicit quality dimensions in grading; two-stage assessment; revision-oriented assessment; multiple acceptable solutions
CATEGORY / THEME: Assessment / quality

1. What is happening here?
The lecturer says that completeness, consistency, and readability are part of evaluation, and that students go through a two-stage submission process with feedback and revision. At the same time, he says he does not explicitly teach model checking.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This suggests a possible gap between teacher-held quality criteria and student-owned evaluation strategies.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Quality is visible in grading, but perhaps less visible as an explicit student self-regulation practice.

4. What evidence supports this?
- “No, model checking, i dont do model checking”
- “they have 2 stages... feedback... final delivery”
- “completeness, consistency, readability… yes… its part of the evaluation”
- “in modelling there is not only one way of modelling”

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Multiple acceptable models are acknowledged, yet quality still has to be judged. This makes quality evaluation more nuanced than simply checking correctness.
