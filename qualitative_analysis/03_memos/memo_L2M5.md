TITLE: Quality is made visible, but student self-evaluation remains weak
DATE: 09/04/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview 2
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: evaluation criteria taught under time pressure; limited student self-evaluation; peer comparison through star rating; peer review bias and competitive pressure; explicit quality criteria; two-stage project assessment; revision supported but not separately graded
CATEGORY / THEME: Assessment / quality

1. What is happening here?
The lecturer explicitly teaches quality criteria, uses a two-stage project structure, provides feedback, and even creates peer comparison opportunities through public star-based classification. Despite that, the lecturer still thinks students do not spend enough time evaluating their own models.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This suggests that making quality visible in assessment is not the same as developing student-owned evaluation habits. Self-evaluation may remain weaker than teacher-led or peer-visible evaluation.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Students may encounter quality mainly as something named by the lecturer or performed in a social comparison setting, rather than as an internal routine during modelling.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "I teach the criteria, but I do not always have enough time"
- "Students do not spend enough time evaluating their own models"
- "compare their work with others and classify the models using stars"
- "it can lead to bias"
- "completeness, consistency, readability, and fit for purpose"
- "There are two stages"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Quality is explicitly articulated and revision is supported, yet the lecturer still sees self-checking as insufficient. Peer review also appears simultaneously helpful and socially biased.

7. Implications
Teaching or tool design may need to externalize quality criteria during modelling itself, not only in grading, peer comparison, or final feedback.

8. Follow-up questions
Do students revise strategically only after feedback, or do they monitor quality while modelling? What forms of self-evaluation support would feel usable rather than punitive?
