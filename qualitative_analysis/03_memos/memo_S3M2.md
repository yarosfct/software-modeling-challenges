TITLE: Notation ambiguity and visual complexity are the main early barriers
DATE: 08/06/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 3
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: ambiguous gateway notation variants; subprocess scope confusion; smooth progress interrupted by unfamiliar syntax; notation as primary initial barrier; visual clutter loses sequence flow; poor layout compounds visual difficulty
CATEGORY / THEME: Learning challenges / notation

1. What is happening here?
Before MSP, the student's main difficulties concerned BPMN notation rather than domain understanding or tools. Ambiguous symbol variants, subprocess boundaries, unfamiliar constructs, and visually dense diagrams all interrupted otherwise smooth progress.

2. Why is this analytically important?
The student explicitly states that notation was the bigger initial problem. That supports treating syntactic variation and visual readability as first-class teaching issues, not side problems once the "real" modelling concepts are taught.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
BPMN offers multiple representational choices and nested structures that are intuitive only after practice. New constructs appear to reset confidence, and layout problems make reading and validating models harder.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "there can be two symbols for the same type of gateway"
- "I wondered whether the subprocess should be considered part of the process I was looking at"
- "until you start to fully understand those details... you will feel stuck a lot of times"
- "the bigger initial problem was notation"
- "When there are many symbols, it is easy to lose track of the sequence flow"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The student also says BPMN is "fairly intuitive" and that tools helped. So the language is approachable at a basic level, but detailed notation and scale create recurring blockages.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
This aligns with student interview 1, where notation and tool overload were experienced together, and student interview 2, where frameworks rather than drawing made modelling difficult. It also resonates with lecturer interview 4's emphasis on meaning over symbol memorisation, but from the learner side the symbols still matter early on.

7. Implications
Teaching should explicitly address alternative symbol forms, subprocess boundaries, and layout discipline early. Progressive syntax introduction, which the student later recommends, fits this barrier pattern.

8. Follow-up questions
Which BPMN constructs most often trigger the "stuck until new part appears" pattern? Do tools reduce or obscure notation ambiguity?
