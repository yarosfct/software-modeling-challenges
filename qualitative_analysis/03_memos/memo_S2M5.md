MEMO ID: S2M5
TITLE: Feedback exists, but mostly if the student actively seeks it
DATE: 23/03/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 2
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: informal professor validation; feedback depends on student initiative; default feedback is just grade; modelling low priority in teaching attention; modelling as one small project component; desire for more staged feedback
CATEGORY / THEME: Feedback / course design

1. What is happening here?
The student describes feedback as available on request, but not strongly built into the modelling task itself. If he asked, a professor could validate the model; otherwise, modelling seemed to end mostly in a grade.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This points to a difference between accessible feedback and structured formative feedback. Students may technically be able to ask for help while still experiencing modelling as under-supported.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Modelling was not treated as a major focal point of the course project, so it received limited dedicated attention unless students themselves pushed for it.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "I showed it to the professor... does this look all right?"
- "if I had a doubt, I could show it to them"
- "if I didn't have the... initiative, it was pretty much just a grade"
- "modeling didn't feel like the main... worry of the professors"
- "model was one of those small parts"
- "if they made more stages... that would have been really helpful"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The student does not describe a hostile or absent teaching environment, but a low-intensity one. Feedback is possible, yet modelling still feels underemphasized.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
This is useful to compare with the lecturer's account of staged assessment and feedback. The student perspective suggests that whatever feedback structure existed was not strongly experienced as modelling-centered support.

7. Implications
If modelling is considered important, it may need more explicit space in the course workflow, clearer checkpoints, and built-in discussion rather than optional consultation.

8. Follow-up questions
How often do students fail to seek feedback even when it is available? Does optional feedback mostly benefit already proactive students?
