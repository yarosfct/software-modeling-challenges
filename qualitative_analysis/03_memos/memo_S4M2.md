MEMO ID: S4M2
TITLE: Private feedback as affective scaffolding for shy students
DATE: 09/06/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 4 (reconstructed)
RELATED RQ(s): RQ1, RQ2, RQ3
RELATED CODES: public sharing stress; private teacher confirmation under ambiguity; need for low-exposure feedback channels; social format shapes modelling experience
CATEGORY / THEME: Feedback, shyness, and participation design

1. What is happening here?
S4 can begin modelling and can reason about quality, but public presentation of half-finished diagrams is stressful. The student prefers email, after-class checks, office hours, or trusted peers.

2. Why is this analytically important?
It complicates the assumption that more public critique is always better. Feedback is necessary, but its format affects who benefits from it.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
The issue is affective safety. Early-stage modelling involves uncertainty; when that uncertainty is exposed publicly, shy students may delay sharing or disengage.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "presenting a half-finished diagram to the whole class is stressful"
- "I prefer to check privately with the teacher if the task is ambiguous"
- "If there were a way to get quick private feedback without public exposure, I would probably share much earlier"
- "the social format of the course matters as much as the notation"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Early feedback is helpful, but public early feedback can inhibit participation. The same activity that helps S5 learn may discourage S4.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
S5 frames public drafts as anxiety-reducing because errors surface early. S4 shows the opposite affective response. Together, they suggest that personality and classroom climate moderate the effect of feedback practices.

7. Implications
Courses could combine public peer review with private or low-stakes channels: anonymous submissions, small-pair reviews, office-hour checkpoints, or quick digital comments before board presentation.

8. Follow-up questions
Which students benefit from public board work, and which avoid it? How can instructors provide critique without increasing exposure anxiety?
