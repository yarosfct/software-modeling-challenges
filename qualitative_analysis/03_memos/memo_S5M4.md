TITLE: Team comprehension as a practical model-quality criterion
DATE: 09/06/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 5 (reconstructed)
RELATED RQ(s): RQ1, RQ2
RELATED CODES: team alignment as quality criterion; onboarding readability check; team comprehension over rubric compliance; role-based model review; stakeholder audience shapes vocabulary
CATEGORY / THEME: Quality as local communicative usefulness

1. What is happening here?
S5 judges model quality by whether the team can use the diagram to agree on what to build, onboard a newcomer, and communicate with stakeholders using their vocabulary.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This student-side quality criterion is practice-oriented. It complements formal correctness with usefulness for coordination and communication.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
The issue is that modelling quality is situated. A model is good enough when it supports the next action for a real audience, not only when it satisfies a rubric.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "the team can point at the diagram and agree on what we are building next"
- "could a new person join tomorrow and understand the feature scope"
- "team comprehension first and rubric second"
- "use their vocabulary, not only ours"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Rubric criteria remain important for grading, but the team uses its own action-oriented standard before submission.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
This aligns with S3 and S4's explainability checks, but S5 adds a team and stakeholder orientation. It also connects to L4's emphasis on stakeholders and communication.

7. Implications
Model-quality teaching could include audience-specific criteria: team coordination, stakeholder understanding, traceability, and assessment compliance.

8. Follow-up questions
Do students who work with real or semi-real stakeholders develop stronger communication-oriented quality criteria?
