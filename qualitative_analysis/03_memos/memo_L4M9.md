MEMO ID: L4M9
TITLE: Students need modelling earlier and with less evaluation overload
DATE: 08/06/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview 4
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: wish for earlier student access; modelling should begin in second year; evaluation overload early in semester; assessment overload limits assimilation; students prefer prescribed tasks under stress; workplace reveals modelling value
CATEGORY / THEME: Curriculum timing / workload

1. What is happening here?
The lecturer wants modelling introduced much earlier, even lightly in programming courses, because students arrive too late and too overloaded to adopt a modelling mindset. Continuous evaluation across multiple courses leaves little space to think, discover, and iterate. Many students only recognise modelling's value later in industry.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This combines two systemic constraints: curricular timing and assessment density. Even strong pedagogy may underperform if students are exhausted and already committed to a programming identity.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Modelling requires reflection, ambiguity tolerance, and iteration, but the degree structure often rewards rapid task closure across parallel assessments. Late exposure then meets a culture of completion rather than exploration.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "Ideally, I would like access to students earlier"
- "they should get some feeling for modelling in the second year"
- "After the fourth or fifth week, they are already stressed with many evaluations"
- "students are overwhelmed with too many grades, projects, tests, and deadlines"
- "students only really understand the need and value of modelling when they are in a working context"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The lecturer uses discovery-based projects while students under pressure ask for prescriptive tasks. The course design assumes mental space that the wider programme may not provide.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
Compared with student interviews 1 and 3, who also experienced modelling as a limited or late part of the curriculum, L4 identifies programme-level workload as a compounding factor. Compared with lecturer interview 3, who noted labs are not substitutes for missed theory, L4 broadens the issue to overall evaluation culture and curricular timing.

7. Implications
Modelling education reforms may need both earlier exposure and protected time for iterative work. Without workload relief, authentic project designs may feel overwhelming rather than enriching.

8. Follow-up questions
Would reducing continuous evaluation increase tolerance for ambiguous projects? What is the lightest viable early modelling intervention in programming courses?
