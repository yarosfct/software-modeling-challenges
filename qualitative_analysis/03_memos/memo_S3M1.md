MEMO ID: S3M1
TITLE: Repeated BPMN exposure creates a head start in MSP
DATE: 08/06/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 3
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: BPMN learned across three courses; prior BPMN contact creates head start; skip basics and focus on advanced notation; immediate start in MSP exercises; prior experience offsets course time pressure; multiple contact points best learning support
CATEGORY / THEME: Prior exposure / spiral curriculum

1. What is happening here?
The student learned BPMN in three courses before and during MSP, but only MSP went deep. The earlier crash courses still gave enough familiarity that the student could start exercises immediately, help a less-prepared colleague, and skip basic assembly work to focus on harder notation.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This is one of the clearest student-side arguments for spiral or distributed modelling exposure rather than a single intensive course. The benefit is not only knowledge retention but reduced dependence on teacher demonstration and faster entry into productive practice.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Initial modelling difficulty is partly a novelty problem. When notation and tool basics are already partially known, students can use class time for refinement, feedback, and harder constructs instead of waiting for scaffolding.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "I learned BPMN in three different courses"
- "Because I already had those two previous contacts with BPMN, I felt much more ahead than my colleague"
- "I could skip over a whole part of learning how to assemble the basics and focus instead on the new symbols"
- "In MSP, I remember clearly that I could start the exercises as soon as the professor showed them"
- "What helped me the most with BPMN was having multiple points of contact with it"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The student values repeated exposure, but two of the three prior contacts were very shallow and one-week long. Spiral learning helped even when earlier teaching was not deep, which suggests frequency and continuity may matter as much as depth in early passes.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
Compared with student interview 2, where starting independently was a barrier, S3 shows how prior exposure can remove that barrier. Compared with lecturer interview 4, who wishes students had modelling earlier, S3 provides student evidence that earlier and repeated contact changes later course experience materially.

7. Implications
Curriculum design could distribute light BPMN or other modelling introductions across related courses rather than relying on MSP alone. Even brief prior contact may pay off if students meet the main modelling course with tool and symbol familiarity.

8. Follow-up questions
Do students without prior BPMN exposure report the same need to wait for examples? How much prior exposure is enough to produce the head-start effect?
