MEMO ID: S5M2
TITLE: High-comfort students learn through public critique and early sharing
DATE: 09/06/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 5 (reconstructed)
RELATED RQ(s): RQ1, RQ2
RELATED CODES: public questioning exposes weak rationale; embarrassing feedback as productive; high comfort sharing drafts; early exposure reduces anxiety; feedback enables risk-taking
CATEGORY / THEME: Public feedback and learner disposition

1. What is happening here?
S5 frequently shares work in labs and sees public drafts as useful. A moment of embarrassment at the board becomes productive because teacher questions exposed gaps in the student's reasoning.

2. Why is this analytically important?
The case demonstrates that active, public, critique-based pedagogy can work very well for some students, especially those comfortable with exposure and peer discussion.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
The underlying issue is learner disposition and classroom climate. The same feedback event can be experienced as humiliation, challenge, or support depending on the student's comfort and team norms.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "I drew something on the board thinking it was fine, and the teacher asked three questions I could not answer"
- "I was embarrassed, but it was useful"
- "Very comfortable"
- "public drafts reduce anxiety because errors surface early"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Public critique is both emotionally risky and pedagogically powerful. The tension becomes visible when compared with S4's avoidance of public half-finished models.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
L4's whiteboard and devil's-advocate approach resonates with S5. However, S4 suggests such methods need complementary private feedback channels.

7. Implications
Teaching design should preserve public discussion but diversify participation modes so that public critique does not become the only path to feedback.

8. Follow-up questions
How can instructors identify when public critique is productive versus discouraging? What norms make public draft sharing safe?
