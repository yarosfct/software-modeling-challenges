TITLE: Domain understanding is a major hidden part of modelling difficulty
DATE: 09/04/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview 2
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: domain understanding as prerequisite; student choice creates uneven complexity; common domain for fairness; current-technology domain selection; theory-to-case and cross-domain transfer fragility
CATEGORY / THEME: Contextual factors / assignment design

1. What is happening here?
The lecturer says that domain understanding is one of the main conditions shaping whether students can model well. Because domain choice can create very uneven project complexity, the lecturer prefers a common domain for all students.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This shifts some of the explanation for modelling difficulty away from notation alone. Students may fail partly because they do not yet understand the problem space well enough to model it.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Modelling appears tightly coupled to domain interpretation. When the domain changes, or when students choose domains with very different complexity, both learning and assessment become harder.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "Students need to understand the domain"
- "it led to unbalanced projects"
- "it is fairer to have one common domain"
- "I usually choose something related to current technologies"
- "They still struggle when the domain changes a little"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Student choice could increase ownership, but it also introduces fairness and comparability problems. Simpler common domains help control difficulty, but may reduce personalization.

7. Implications
Assignment design matters greatly. Beginners may need domains that are both understandable and comparable, especially when modelling is being assessed.
