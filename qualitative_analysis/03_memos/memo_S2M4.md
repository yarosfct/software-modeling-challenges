TITLE: Quality is judged through simplicity, completeness, and message clarity
DATE: 23/03/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 2
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: completeness and simplicity as quality criteria; understandable at a glance; benchmark by simplicity and understandability; modelling with a specific message goal; good modeller creates clear simplicity; intuitive understandability
CATEGORY / THEME: Quality criteria

1. What is happening here?
The student evaluates model quality mainly in terms of completeness, simplicity, and whether the model clearly conveys the intended message.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This shows that students may already use a communicative quality logic even when they do not remember formal quality rules or checklists.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Quality is framed less as formal compliance and more as purposeful communication. A good model captures what matters without becoming harder to read.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "I have two main ones, which is completeness and simplicity"
- "can understand... what's going on"
- "if I feel like mine is as simple and... understandable as theirs"
- "I want to convey a message"
- "something that you can look at and... intuitively understand"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The student values completeness, but not at the expense of simplicity. This may create trade-offs that are not fully externalized in course guidance.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
This is close to student interview 1, where readability and clarity also anchored quality. It also echoes the lecturer's emphasis on readability, though the student foregrounds message clarity more strongly here.

7. Implications
Quality support might work best if it starts from communicative intent: what message should this model convey, to whom, and with how much detail?

8. Follow-up questions
Do students usually begin by asking what the message of the model is, or do they arrive at this only after experience? How does message clarity interact with assessment criteria?
