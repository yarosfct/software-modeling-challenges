MEMO ID: S3M5
TITLE: Feedback and complete examples matter more than slides alone
DATE: 08/06/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 3
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: waits for professor feedback to confirm correctness; needed worked example before starting in crash course; incomplete examples in theory slides; slides alone insufficient without feedback; complete examples in theory before advanced labs; theory-practice alignment needed
CATEGORY / THEME: Feedback / scaffolding

1. What is happening here?
The student consistently relies on professor feedback to feel sure about model correctness, especially early on. When classes were missed, slides alone were insufficient because they lacked complete examples and could not confirm whether the student's interpretation was right. The student's main teaching recommendation is to connect theory slides to practice through complete examples before introducing harder syntax in labs.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This links three issues that often appear separately: feedback dependence, inadequate static materials, and theory-practice misalignment. The student is not asking only for more time, but for better-sequenced support that reduces uncertainty before practice.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Modelling correctness is hard to infer from incomplete materials. Without live validation or fully worked examples, students cannot tell whether they are practicing the right patterns, even if they believe they are following the slides.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "I definitely waited for feedback from the professor"
- "I had to wait until the professor gave an example"
- "The theoretical slides do not always include examples, or they include an example that does not show the full syntax"
- "Reading the slides alone created problems for me"
- "Complete examples would be very useful"
- "the practical exercises should be better connected to the theoretical slides"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The student could start MSP exercises quickly because of prior BPMN exposure, yet still wanted professor feedback for certainty. Confidence to begin does not eliminate the need for validation.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
Compared with student interview 2, where feedback existed but often had to be sought actively, S3 describes feedback as routine and necessary. Compared with lecturer interview 4, who avoids giving solutions upfront and uses whiteboard discussion, S3 suggests students still need earlier exemplars of correct practice in theory materials before being left to discover through struggle.

7. Implications
Course design should pair exploratory practice with complete reference examples and reliable feedback channels. Improving slides alone is insufficient if examples are partial or validation is absent.

8. Follow-up questions
Would complete worked examples reduce dependence on professor walk-arounds? How do shy students get enough feedback when they avoid public sharing?
