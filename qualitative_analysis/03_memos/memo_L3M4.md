TITLE: Labs privilege autonomy first, with intervention built around decomposition
DATE: 09/04/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview 3
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: no rigid process for every diagram; learn-by-doing with typed tools; strongly typed tools prevent invalid operations; individual warm-up before group work; breaking problems into smaller parts; one-piece board demonstration; similar-example scaffolding; autonomy-first support strategy; intervention when majority stuck; pre-emptive worked examples for hard labs; written lab guides and slide signposting
CATEGORY / THEME: Scaffolding

1. What is happening here?
The lecturer does not describe one rigid modelling procedure for all diagram types. Instead, labs are organized around practice, typed tools, small warm-up exercises, and selective intervention when students get stuck. A key support move is breaking a difficult problem into smaller modelable pieces.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This offers a more nuanced picture of scaffolding. Students are not simply shown complete solutions; support is staged, threshold-based, and often focused on decomposition rather than full teacher takeover.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Students need enough autonomy to attempt modelling themselves, but they also need help making large problems manageable. The lecturer's support strategy tries to preserve both.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "Not in a rigid step-by-step way for every diagram"
- "learn by doing, supported by adequate tools"
- "students started with individual warm-up exercises"
- "break the problem into smaller parts"
- "show on the board how that piece could be modeled"
- "Only when the majority of students were stuck would we intervene"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The course promotes autonomy, yet prepared examples, guides, and whole-class interventions remain essential when difficulty rises.

7. Implications
Support for modelling may be most effective when it helps students decompose and restart rather than simply handing them a finished model.
