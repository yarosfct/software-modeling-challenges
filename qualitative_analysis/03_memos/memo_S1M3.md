MEMO ID: S1M3
TITLE: Poor group dynamics can push modelling out of the workflow
DATE: 23/03/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 1
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: unequal group contribution; task delegation frustration; modelling avoidance after frustration; implementation pressure displaces modelling; preference for smaller teams; shared model for team alignment
CATEGORY / THEME: Group work / contextual factors

1. What is happening here?
The student's most negative modelling memory is tied to a large group project with uneven participation. Under these conditions, modelling and project structuring were experienced less as support and more as overhead, and the student says he later avoided modelling.

2. Why is this analytically important?
It suggests that negative attitudes toward modelling may partly come from social and organizational conditions, not only from notation or conceptual difficulty.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
When accountability is weak and deadlines matter, modelling may be abandoned in favor of direct implementation. However, the student still believes modelling could have helped everyone share the same picture of the project.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "half of them didn't really do much"
- "the task delegation ended up being extra frustrating"
- "after that, I pretty much avoided modeling"
- "work had to be done, I just sort of got to it"
- "modeling things would be helpful, because everybody would have a clear picture"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Modelling is blamed as part of a frustrating project process, yet the student also says it could have improved shared understanding. The problem may be less modelling itself than the context in which it was used.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
Compare with the lecturer's emphasis on student passivity and course context. Here, the student adds group size, low participation, and coordination burdens as part of the modelling experience.

7. Implications
Research on modelling learning should not isolate modelling from teamwork and project organization. Support around collaboration may change whether modelling is seen as useful.

8. Follow-up questions
Would modelling be perceived differently in individual work or smaller groups? How often do students associate modelling difficulties with team frustration?
