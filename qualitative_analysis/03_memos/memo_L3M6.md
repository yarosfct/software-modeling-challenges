TITLE: Continuous evaluation works better, but revision-rich feedback is constrained by scale and breadth
DATE: 09/04/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview 3
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: continuous evaluation plus strong individual component; continuous evaluation improves performance; multi-stage submissions across diagram types; no revision of same artefact due time pressure; scale makes rich feedback difficult; broad UML coverage; large-scale modelling course
CATEGORY / THEME: Assessment / contextual factors

1. What is happening here?
The lecturer reports better student performance under continuous evaluation than under an exam-only route. However, the course does not support ideal revision loops on the same models because the class is large, UML coverage is broad, and feedback at scale is time-intensive.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This shows that assessment design is not just a pedagogical choice; it is also constrained by class size, topic breadth, and the labor of feedback.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
The lecturer values iterative learning, but the available time has to be distributed across many diagram types and many students. As a result, multi-stage assessment exists, yet not in the fully revision-oriented way the lecturer would prefer.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "continuous evaluation with assignments developed in groups"
- "individual assessment component worth at least 50 percent"
- "the continuous-evaluation group performed clearly better"
- "with multiple submissions, probably around four"
- "we usually did not return to use case modeling later"
- "doing that manually at scale is very difficult for professors"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Continuous evaluation appears more effective, yet the richer version of continuous feedback the lecturer wants is not feasible under current conditions.

7. Implications
Automated or semi-automated feedback may be especially valuable in large modelling courses if it can support revision without adding unmanageable teaching load.
