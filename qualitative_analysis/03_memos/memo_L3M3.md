TITLE: Semantic rigor through executability comes with a heavy OCL burden
DATE: 09/04/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview 3
RELATED RQ(s): 
RELATED CODES: OCL learning curve; executable modelling depends on OCL; constraint language as major difficulty; uneven student distribution; demanding course perception
CATEGORY / THEME: Learning challenges / tooling

1. What is happening here?
The lecturer says that executable modelling depended on students learning OCL, and that this was one of the biggest sources of difficulty in the course. Students were unevenly distributed in how well they handled that demand, and many experienced the course as very challenging.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This shows that a more semantically rigorous tool can improve assessment and validation while simultaneously raising the barrier to entry for students.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
There is a tradeoff between expressive power and learnability. Students are not only learning diagrams; they are also being asked to learn a formal constraint language in order to achieve executability.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "students usually do not learn deeply, namely OCL"
- "without it they could not build executable models"
- "one of the biggest sources of difficulty in the course"
- "the learning curve was too steep"
- "About one third were very good students... The other two thirds often felt the course was too demanding"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The tool improves semantic rigor, but that rigor makes the course harder for many students. What is pedagogically valuable is also what may alienate a large part of the cohort.

7. Implications
If executable modelling is used, students may need stronger support for constraint expression and clearer bridges between natural language and formal semantics.

8. Follow-up questions
Could executable modelling be introduced more gradually? Are there lighter ways to expose students to semantic checking without requiring full OCL mastery?
