
1. Hi, my name is Yaroslav Hayduk. And this is my thesis first-stage presentation. The thesis is about the Challenges related to learning and teaching Software modelling, using a socio-technical grounded theory approach

2. For context, software modelling is taught across many CS courses, 
but students often don't experience it as a meaningful engineering activity, and instead they find it to be something they are forced to do, because it is present in the curricula.
At the sametime instructors they are constrained by time, grading load, and the ability to give feedback. 
Additionally the used tools and course setup -like the tasks used, domain choice, feedback practices- can greatly impact the perceived value of modelling.

3. There is substantial work on modelling education, but its fragmented across different communities and contexts. Many studies focus on a single notation, tool, or course intervention. What is missing is an integrated, empirically grounded explanation connecting:

what challenges happen in modelling work,
what kinds of supports people use or need, and
what conditions shape those experiences, those conditions being, tools, tasks, feedback, assessment or domain context.

4. My goal is to build an empirically grounded explanation of what challenges occur in learning and teaching software modelling, and what support needs address those challenges. With explicit emphasis on the socio-technical aspect of those conditions.

5. I have 3 main RQs, starting with the first one

6. What Challenges are perceived across modelling activities? The goal here is to understand and map in which modelling activities students struggle the most. Those activities can range from simple model interpretation to advanced model creation and refinment.

7. The second RQ is: What strategies and support needs address these challenges? Here the goal is to figure out what can be done to mitigate those challenges, either it being changes to the course desing or tool-related support.

8. The third RQ: How do socio-technical factors shape experiences? the aim of this rq is to understand how socio-technical factors, like course context, domain choice for the modelling tasks, group work, tool choice, how the feedback is perceived, and so on, influence the emergence and handling of challenges.

9. I'm using related work mainly to position the gap, not to list everything. The existing work that i reviewed falls into 3 buckets

Outcome frameworks, those describe modelling competencies and inteded outcomes (what students should be able to do)
Empirical studies that report recurring student difficulties
Lastly work focused on tools and course practices, such as feedback, tooling, assessment approaches.
My argument is that we have valuable resources, but we still lack an integrated explanatory account that links these buckets together in a grounded way.


10. To develop that integrated explanation, i will be using a grounded theory approach with a socio-technical orientation. This method is iterative and some stages will occur in parallel. This GT approach fits my context because my goal is to build an explanation/theory from data, instead of testing a predefined hypothesis. 
The process will be the following: I will start with data collection, that in my case will be interviews students and instructors, and an small modelling tasks that will ask students to do. Then with that data i will start the coding process, then analysis and grounded explanation. I will explain those process in more detail in a later slide, so we dont lose too much time here.

11. Now i will quickly describe the study desing process. As i said previously it all starts with the data collection, which in this case we have 2 approaches. The semi structured interviews and modelling tasks for students. With this data we can move onto the triangulation process

12. This triangulation process is important for the coding and analysis aspects of GT, here my goal will be to identify, compare and refine emerging concepts, patterns and interpretations, which then will be carefully analysed 

13. This analysis will follow a specific framework, the data will be analyzed through socio technical lens and using grounded theory logic, and will focus on conditions, actions/strategies and consequences, this way all 3 RQs can be fulfilled.

14. The analysis plan follows the GT methodology, as those concepts can somewhat unclear at first i would like you to think of one example: a student says that he can draw classes but struggle choosing relationships, leading to inconsistent models.

In open coding, I would label that incident with codes like relationship selection, semantic uncertainty, inconsistency maybe.
In axial coding i would relate it as a link between conditions -> actions and outcomes, a possible condition would be limited guidance or tool friction, thent the action would be guessing or copying patterns which will result on a outcome, that is low confidence and poor model quality.
In selective coding, i integrate repeated patterns into an explanatory account, like lack of semantic criteria / feedback loop as a core aspect of that issue.
Bloom is only a communication vocabulary, here the issue is understand/apply/evalute, not just remembering notation.

Possible questions


Why Grounded Theory?
“Because the goal is an integrated explanation in a context-dependent, under-theorised area. GT is designed for theory-building from experience-near data, with iterative sampling as categories emerge.”

What does socio-technical mean here?
“It means modelling experience is shaped not just by notation knowledge, but by tools, tasks, assessment, feedback, and domain context, and the interactions between them.”

How will you decide sample size / when to stop?
“Through category saturation or sufficient category elaboration—when new interviews stop adding properties/relationships to the key categories.”

How do you avoid bias / forcing Bloom?
“Coding is inductive. Bloom is only a reporting vocabulary later to communicate cognitive character, not a template that drives coding.”

How will you compare across different tools/courses?
“By systematically capturing context metadata and using constant comparison. Variability becomes part of the explanation rather than noise.”

What will the output look like?
“A set of linked categories—conditions, strategies, outcomes—integrated into an explanatory account, plus concrete implications for teaching practices and tool support.”
