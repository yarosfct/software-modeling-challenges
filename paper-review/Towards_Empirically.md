# Maslov — "Towards Empirically Validated Process Modelling Education Using a BPMN Formalism


## 1. Problem and Motivation (Sec. 1 — Context and Motivation)

- The paper focuses on **process modelling education** using **BPMN** as the main formalism.
- BPMN is a **de-facto standard** in industry for documenting and analysing business processes;
  good models are crucial for understanding, automation and continuous improvement.
- However:
  - **Process modelling (PM) education is unsystematic** and oftem based on **individual teachers' experience** rather than an agreed framework.
  - Existing teaching is **imbalanced across Bloom levels:** lots of remembering/understanding/applying, big gaps in **scaffolding, evaluation and higher-order skills**.
  - Despite large bodies of research on **conceptual modelling education** and **process model quality,** there is **little integration** betwen them and **few empirically validated pedagogical approaches** for BOMN courses.


**Goal of the research project (this paper is a roadmap/ first step):**

> Move **towards an empirically validated educational framework** for BPMN-based process modelling, grounded in CM education theory, learning sciences, and process model quality research.


## 2. Background: What We Already Know (Sec. 1-3. literature synthesis)

Maslov builds on a **systematic literature review of conceptual modelling education** and links it to **process modelling**. Key ideas the paper imports / aggregates:

- **Student factors that matter for CM/PM learning:**
  - **Intelligence and cognitive abilities —** influence how easiliy learners grasp abstractions and apply new information.
  - **Cognitive style —** e.g., preferences for certain representations; linked to how students read and build process models. 
  - **Personalitiy and intrinsic motivation —** strong predictors of engagement and learning outcomes.
  - **Academic discipline / background —** students from different programmes may understand and value CM differently.
- **Course and pedagogy factors:**
  - Need for **clear learning goals** and scaffolding of modelling skills (not just syntax).
  - Importance of **formative assessment** and **automated feedback** on model quality.
  - Potential of **e-learning / MOOCs**, learning analytics and adaptive systems to provide feedback at scale.
  - Influence of **different quality frameworks** (e.g., syntatic / semantic / pragmatic quality) and **process model quality metrics** on what we should teach and how we should assess.
- **Existing solutions:**
  - Prior work was proposed:
    - adaptive teaching based on **cognitive profiles**,
    - **learning ontologies** that link objectives, items and errors for formative feedback,
    - automated BPMN checkers giving immediate feedback on quality issues,
    - various quality frameworks and guidelines for good process models.
  - But these are **not yet integrated** into a comprehensive, empirically validated BPMN education framework.

## 3. Research Objectives and Questions (Sec. 3-4)

The paper frames a **multi-stage research programme** with three main objectives / RQs:

### 3.1 RO1 / RQ1 — Map the existing knowledge base

- **Goal:** Systematically map research on **process modelling education** and its connection to **conceptual modelling education**, focusing on BPMN.
- **Method (planned):** a **bibliometric and content-analysis-based SLR** of publications (approx. 2000-2021), to identify:
  - main topics,
  - educational contexts,
  - methods,
  - open gaps and under-researched aspects.

(This part is "currently in progress" and reported in a separate paper; here it's described as the foundation.)

### 3.2 RO2 / RQ2 — Understanding novice modellers' behaviour in BPMN

- **Goal:** Analyse **students' / novice modellers' behaviour** when creating BPMN models, with a focus on:
  - typical **errors and quality issues,**
  - how these relate to **learning styles, prior knowledge and cognitive factors,**
  - how behaviour evolves during learning.
- **Planned approach:** empirical studies that collect **BPMN models produced by learners,** assess them with process model quality frameworks and (possibly) log data from tools, in order to connect **model characteristics** with **learner characteristics** and **pedagogical conditions.**

### 3.3 RO3 / RQ3 — Design and empirically validate BPMN pedagogy

- **Goal:** Use insights from RO1 and RO2 to **design, implement and empirically evaluate:**
  - BPMN-specific **learning goals,**
  - **course design and teaching strategies,**
  - **feedback mechanisms** (e.g., automated feedback, learning analytics dashboards),
  - potentially **real-time model simulation** as a learning aid.
- **Methodological stance: Design Science Research** combining:
  - theoretical framing from learning sciences (behaviourism, constructivism, cognitivism, multimodal learning, etc.),
  - iterative design + experiment cycles,
  - emphasis on **empirical validation** (not just proposing frameworks).

## 4. Proposed Theoretical and Methodological Framework (Sec. 3-4)

The paper sketches the **theoretical backbone** that will guide the empirical work:

- Draws on **learning theories** (behaviourism, constructivism, cognitivism) plus more recent **digital and multimodal pedagogy** (e.g., Cope and Kalantzis' reflexive pedagogy).
- Envisions a framework that supports:
  - **individualised learning paths,**
  - adaptive difficulty based on learner profile and knowledge level,
  - **recursive feedback loops** (students receive feedback on model, update them, reflect),
  - a mix of **digital tools, e-learning components and traditional teaching.**
- Existing CM-education solutions (e.g., MOOC-based formative assessment, cognitive-profile-based training, automated BPMN feedback) are treated as **building blocks** that can be integrated and empirically evaluated in the BPMN context.

For my thesis this is relevant as **meta-related work:** it shows how people are starting to **bridge CM education theory, process model quality, and pedagogy** in a more systematic way.

## 5. Conclusion and Future Work (Sec. 4)

- Process modelling education is presented as a **complex, multi-factor phenomenon** requiring input from:
  - conceptual/process modelling research,
  - learning theories,
  - educational technology,
  - process model quality frameworks.
- The paper **does not yet presnt empirical results;** instead, it:
  - articulates the **research agenda** (RO1-RO3),
  - justifies the chosen **design-science methodology,**
  - positions ongoing work (SLR and planned experiments).
- Ultimate long-term aim:
  > to move towards an **empirically validated educational framework** for BPMN process modelling that defines learning goals, content, teaching strategies and feedback mechanisms grounded in data.

## 6. Thesis-ready one-paragraph summary

Maslov outlines a research agenda for process modelling education centered on BPMN and grounded in prior work on conceptual modelling education, process model quality and learning sciences. The papers argues that current BPMN teaching is largely unsystematic, based on individual instructurs' experience and imbalanced across cognitive levels, despite the availability of research on conceptual modelling pedagogy and automated quality assessment. To address this, the author proposes a multi-stage programme: first, a bibliometric and content-analytic review of process-modelling education research; second, empirical studies analysing novice modellers' behaviour and typical BPMN model quality issues; and third, the design and experimental validation of BPMN-specific learning goals, course designs and feedback mechanisms, potentially including real-time simulation and adaptive e-learning components. Framed within a design-science methodology and informed by contemporary pedagogical theories, the project aims to develop an empirically validated educational framework for BPMN that systematically links learner characteristics, teaching strategies and model-quality outcomes.
