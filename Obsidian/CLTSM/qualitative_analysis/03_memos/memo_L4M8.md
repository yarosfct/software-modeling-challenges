MEMO ID: L4M8
TITLE: Soft skills and empathy are part of requirements modelling competence
DATE: 08/06/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview 4
RELATED RQ(s): RQ1, RQ2, RQ3
RELATED CODES: communication and empathy as core skills; informal contact builds stakeholder trust; icebreakers build classroom trust; role-play stakeholder interviews in class; refuses to give direct exercise solutions
CATEGORY / THEME: Socio-technical skills

1. What is happening here?
The lecturer treats communication, empathy, and trust-building as essential to requirements modelling, not optional extras. Professional examples include informal contact with operational staff after top-down entry created resistance. In class, icebreakers, openness, and role-play aim to make students comfortable enough to listen, question, and revise models critically.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This challenges a technical framing of modelling education. Getting the right model depends on how students and practitioners relate to stakeholders, not only on notation competence.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Requirements remain unsaid when people feel threatened or uncomfortable. Modelling competence therefore includes relational skills that enable elicitation and validation.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "Communication is very important... empathy"
- "If you cannot see the other person... you will not get the right requirements"
- "I would meet them during coffee breaks and chat with them"
- "I use icebreakers in the first class"
- "One team plays the role of stakeholders, while another plays developers"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Soft skills are emphasised strongly, yet assessment and student culture still lean toward technical task completion. Students may want solutions while the lecturer models openness and critical dialogue.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
Compared with student interview 3, who sought professor feedback and was shy about public sharing, L4 tries to build the trust and communication channels that make feedback and elicitation possible. Compared with lecturer interview 2, where domain understanding was a hidden difficulty, L4 adds relational access to domain knowledge as a core competence.

IMPLICATIONS:
Requirements and modelling courses should teach interview skills, empathy, and trust-building alongside notation. Role-play and icebreakers are not peripheral; they support model quality.

OPEN QUESTIONS:
Which soft-skill activities do students find most valuable? Do students recognise empathy as part of modelling competence or as separate professionalism?
