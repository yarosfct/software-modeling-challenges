MEMO ID: S6M1
TITLE: The missing bridge between early requirements models and UML
DATE: 09/06/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 6 (reconstructed)
RELATED RQ(s): RQ1, RQ2, RQ3
RELATED CODES: transition from goal model to UML as hardest moment; syntactic correctness without goal traceability; missing goal-to-class trace; unclear translation criteria between model families; missing cross-model methodology
CATEGORY / THEME: Cross-model integration and traceability

1. What is happening here?
S6's hardest moment was not drawing iStar or UML separately, but translating an iStar rationale model into UML while preserving the goals and rationale.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This is a strong case for the thesis claim that modelling education must address integration across representations, not only individual notations.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
The underlying issue is a gap in procedural methodology: students know two model families but lack guidance for how meaning should travel from one to the other.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "the class diagram looked syntactically correct but lost the goals"
- "the trace from goals to classes was missing"
- "nobody had explicitly explained how much of the iStar model should survive in UML"
- "the real pain was the missing methodology for translation between the models"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
A model can be syntactically correct and still fail as a requirements artefact if rationale and traceability disappear.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
L4 teaches early-to-late requirements and emphasises integration, rationale, and traceability. S6 provides a student-side view of why that integration remains difficult.

IMPLICATIONS:
Courses should include continuous worked examples that explicitly show transformation from goals to NFRs to UML, with trace links and decisions documented.

OPEN QUESTIONS:
What concrete translation rules or artefacts help students preserve rationale across model families?
