MEMO ID: L2M3
TITLE: Process teaching is explicit, but modelling is not reducible to a recipe
DATE: 09/04/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview 2
RELATED RQ(s): RQ1, RQ2
RELATED CODES: explicit step-by-step process; modelling requires situated decisions; no universal checklist; example-first teaching; autonomy-first exercises; uneven student autonomy
CATEGORY / THEME: Scaffolding / epistemic view of modelling

1. What is happening here?
The lecturer says that a step-by-step process is taught, but also insists that modelling always requires case-specific decisions. Teaching alternates between worked examples and more autonomous exercises.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This reveals an important tension in modelling pedagogy. Students need procedural support, but the lecturer does not see modelling as something that can be fully encoded into a fixed checklist.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
There is a tension between the need for structure and the situated nature of modelling judgment. Beginners may want a recipe, while the lecturer wants them to learn flexible decision-making.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "Yes, I teach a step-by-step process"
- "they still have to make many decisions"
- "there is no checklist that solves everything"
- "first I show an example"
- "sometimes I only give them the exercise and expect them to do it more autonomously"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The lecturer teaches process explicitly, but rejects the idea that process alone can determine the best model. That creates a difficult balance for novice learners.

IMPLICATIONS:
Pedagogical or tool support may need to scaffold modelling decisions without pretending that one universal procedure always fits.

OPEN QUESTIONS:
What kind of guidance can preserve judgment while still giving beginners a reliable starting structure? Can prompts be adaptive rather than checklist-like?
