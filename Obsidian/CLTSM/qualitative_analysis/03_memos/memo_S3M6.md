MEMO ID: S3M6
TITLE: Motivation shapes speed, but audience-aware readability shapes care
DATE: 08/06/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 3
RELATED RQ(s): RQ3
RELATED CODES: motivation affects speed more than quality; no felt real-world impact of thesis models; analyst audience motivates modelling care; layout for eye-tracker readability; BPMN more visible in military than industry; difficulty recruiting BPMN-knowledgeable participants
CATEGORY / THEME: Motivation / communication

1. What is happening here?
The student says domain motivation affects work speed more than final quality, and did not feel that thesis models would change real systems. Yet when other people would analyse the model, especially in an eye-tracking experiment, the student deliberately improved readability through spacing and layout. The student also notes weak professional BPMN visibility outside specific sectors such as the military.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This complicates the authenticity argument. Even without real-world deployment, a known audience can motivate careful modelling. Conversely, weak professional visibility may reduce the sense that modelling matters beyond university.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Motivation operates through different channels: task completion, experimental rigour, and professional relevance. The student can maintain quality through disciplined self-review even when motivation is low, but extra care appears when model readability affects others' analysis.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "Motivation definitely impacts the speed of work... but I would not say it impacts the final quality that much"
- "the models themselves were not going to be integrated into systems"
- "I felt that the way I modelled would affect how other people analysed the model"
- "I tried to separate the elements on the screen as much as possible"
- "BPMN is used more by armies than by industry"
- "a problem for me when trying to find volunteers... who knew BPMN"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The student first downplays real-world impact, then revises that view when analyst interpretation is considered. Modelling care exists, but its source is audience and method needs rather than operational deployment.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
Compared with student interview 1, where authentic and personally meaningful domains increased effort, S3 is less domain-driven but still audience-driven. Compared with lecturer interview 4, who tries to show practical value through stakeholder work and later workplace recognition, S3 suggests students may not feel that value during studies unless the audience or use case is immediate and visible.

IMPLICATIONS:
Assignments can increase modelling care by giving students a concrete reader, reviewer, or analyst rather than assuming real-world impact alone will motivate them. Professional visibility of notation in industry may also affect perceived worth.

OPEN QUESTIONS:
Does audience-aware layout transfer to non-research assignments? Would workplace examples increase motivation more than domain choice?
