TITLE: Living diagrams as group coordination infrastructure
DATE: 09/06/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 5 (reconstructed)
RELATED RQ(s): RQ1, RQ2, RQ3
RELATED CODES: living diagrams on collaborative board; shared model ownership; routine model maintenance meeting; model prevents code-level coordination conflict; model maintenance overhead
CATEGORY / THEME: Collaborative modelling practice

1. What is happening here?
S5 describes a team that used Miro as a living modelling board during a campus event app project. The model remained active beyond the graded requirement and supported weekly coordination.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This is a positive counter-case to S4. It shows that modelling can become meaningful when it is embedded into team routines and shared ownership.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
The issue is integration of modelling into workflow. When diagrams are updated, reviewed, and used in decisions, they become coordination infrastructure rather than documentation.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "we kept a shared Miro board with living diagrams"
- "modelling continued as a habit for that group"
- "weekly short diagram stand-up"
- "Without the Miro board, we would have argued in code"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Living diagrams help coordination but create maintenance overhead, especially when code changes are merged without updating the model.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
Compared with S4, group culture is the decisive difference. Lecturer interviews also describe models as communication and coordination tools; S5 shows how that value can be experienced by students.

IMPLICATIONS:
Assessment could include small recurring modelling milestones or diagram reviews to encourage models as process artefacts, not final-report decorations.

OPEN QUESTIONS:
What team conditions make living diagrams sustainable? How can tool support reduce maintenance burden?
