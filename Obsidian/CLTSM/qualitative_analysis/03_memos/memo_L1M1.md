MEMO ID: L1M1
TITLE: Starting is harder than knowing notation
DATE: 19/03/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview
RELATED RQ(s): RQ1, RQ2, RQ3
RELATED CODES: notation insufficient; break the resistance; break the ice; problem-to-model translation; student insecurity; reliance on teacher initiation
CATEGORY / THEME: Learning challenges

1. What is happening here?
The lecturer repeatedly suggests that students may understand notation when it is explained, but still struggle to begin modelling. The main difficulty is described not as remembering concepts, but as moving from understanding a case to producing a model.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This suggests that modelling learning is not only about syntax or conceptual knowledge. There is a threshold between knowing and doing that seems especially important in modelling education.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Likely a combination of procedural difficulty, insecurity, and low confidence. It may also involve fear of making mistakes and dependence on teacher confirmation.

4. What evidence supports this?
- “they learn the notation but its not enough”
- “they actually have to practice to break the resistance”
- “to break the ice”
- “they wait for the teacher to start the solution”

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Students are expected to become independent modellers, yet many appear to rely heavily on teacher initiation.

IMPLICATIONS:
Educational support may need to explicitly address initiation, first steps, and confidence-building rather than focusing only on explaining notation.

OPEN QUESTIONS:
Do students also experience this as insecurity? Does this appear across different modelling notations?
