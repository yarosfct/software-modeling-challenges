MEMO ID: S6M2
TITLE: Ambiguity becomes manageable when alternatives can be justified
DATE: 09/06/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 6 (reconstructed)
RELATED RQ(s): RQ1, RQ2
RELATED CODES: multiple defensible iStar decompositions; document alternatives and justify choice; trade-off documentation reduces anxiety; vague briefs create initial stress; modelling lacks compile-button certainty
CATEGORY / THEME: Ambiguity tolerance and justification

1. What is happening here?
S6 initially finds vague requirements stressful, but the course reduces anxiety by teaching students to document alternatives, make trade-offs explicit, and justify choices.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This case shows a pedagogical response to the lack of single correct answers in modelling. Instead of eliminating ambiguity, the course teaches students to work with it.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Students trained in programming contexts may expect a definitive answer or compile-button feedback. Modelling requires comfort with defensible alternatives and incomplete certainty.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "different actor decompositions or dependency directions can be defensible"
- "documented alternatives in a short note, picked one, and justified it"
- "explicit trade-offs ... reduced my exam anxiety"
- "there is no compile button"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Vague briefs are stressful but pedagogically necessary. The challenge is to provide enough structure for justification without making the problem artificially closed.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
This relates to lecturer practices of giving ambiguous problem descriptions and requiring stakeholder elicitation. It also contrasts with students who seek teacher confirmation for correctness.

IMPLICATIONS:
Assessment can reward rationale and alternatives, not just final diagrams. This may support ambiguity tolerance and reduce fear of multiple valid answers.

OPEN QUESTIONS:
Does explicit grading of alternatives reduce student dependence on teacher confirmation?
