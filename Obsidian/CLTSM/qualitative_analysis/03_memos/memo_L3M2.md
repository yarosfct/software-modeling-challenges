TITLE: Executable modelling makes models dynamic learning and assessment artifacts
DATE: 09/04/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview 3
RELATED RQ(s): RQ2, RQ3
RELATED CODES: executable UML as distinctive assessment approach; model execution makes modelling dynamic; executable models support grading validation; open-source student-accessible tool; tool chosen for grading support
CATEGORY / THEME: Assessment / tooling

1. What is happening here?
The lecturer places strong value on executable UML and the USE environment because students can run their models and teachers can validate what students produced through execution rather than static inspection alone.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This is a distinctive view of modelling pedagogy. The model is not just a communication artifact or diagram to be read visually; it becomes something testable and behaviorally inspectable.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Executability seems to reduce ambiguity in both learning and grading. If the model can be instantiated and run, students get more concrete feedback and lecturers get stronger evidence for assessment.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "it relied on executable UML"
- "students could see the models executing"
- "the models became dynamic rather than static"
- "it helped us validate what students had produced"
- "It helped a lot with grading"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The same features that make executable modelling pedagogically powerful also increase complexity, especially because they rely on OCL.

IMPLICATIONS:
Executable modelling may offer a strong route for making model meaning more explicit, especially in assessment and feedback.

OPEN QUESTIONS:
Do students perceive execution as genuinely helpful for understanding, or mainly as part of grading? Could executability help other modelling families too?
