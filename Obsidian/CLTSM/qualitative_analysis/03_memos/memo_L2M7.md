MEMO ID: L2M7
TITLE: Time-rich modelling pedagogy is constrained by attendance and workload
DATE: 09/04/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview 2
RELATED RQ(s): RQ2, RQ3
RELATED CODES: attendance and competing workload matter; attendance decline linked to social calendar; external materials can mislead through convention mismatch; more teaching time as main need; evaluation and feedback cycles are time-intensive
CATEGORY / THEME: Contextual factors

1. What is happening here?
The lecturer describes modelling learning as strongly shaped by time, attendance, workload, and access to guided class interaction. The most desired improvement is simply more time, especially because good modelling pedagogy involves exercises, feedback, further exercises, and revision.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This broadens the explanation of modelling difficulties beyond student ability or notation complexity. Learning opportunities themselves are unstable because students miss class, juggle other assessments, and sometimes rely on external materials with different conventions.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
The lecturer wants an iterative, feedback-rich process, but the course context does not reliably sustain it. Absence and cross-course pressure reduce the chances for modelling support to work as intended.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "They matter a lot."
- "After the big party here in Beja I lose many students"
- "resources that are not good, or that use different conventions"
- "the first thing I need is more time"
- "continuous assessment with feedback takes a lot of time"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The course expects engagement with modelling practice, but attendance patterns and workload pull students away from the very activities that would help them improve.

IMPLICATIONS:
Any intervention for modelling learning needs to account for limited teacher time and irregular student participation, not only improve explanations or tools.

OPEN QUESTIONS:
Which parts of modelling support are most damaged by absence: concept introduction, guided practice, or feedback? Could some supports survive better outside class than others?
