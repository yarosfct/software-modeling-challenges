TITLE: The student wants examples, templates, and incremental progression
DATE: 23/03/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 1
RELATED RQ(s): RQ1, RQ2, RQ3
RELATED CODES: trial-and-error modelling from text; components-before-actors strategy; limited explicit procedural instruction; earlier iterative feedback would help; preference for example-based support; text-to-model examples as needed support; endorsement of template-based support; desire for incremental modelling instruction; build complexity gradually; same evolving case for continuity
CATEGORY / THEME: Teaching support / scaffolding

1. What is happening here?
The student describes modelling from text as trial and error and has a partial personal strategy for starting. He directly requests examples, earlier feedback, and incremental instruction, and endorses the interviewer's suggestion of templates.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This strongly supports the idea that students need procedural scaffolding, not just conceptual explanation. The student identifies several supports that could make modelling more learnable, although templates were interviewer-prompted.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
The challenge appears to be proceduralizing the move from text to model while managing notation uncertainty. Without visible process support, the student falls back on guess-and-revise.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "I will make a model that is incorrect, and then... go back and forth"
- "it was more just like concepts. Here's the text, get to it"
- "examples for me personally are more useful"
- "this text description, translates to this model"
- interviewer proposal: templates for common modelling steps; student response: "Yes, I think so"
- "more incremental approach to the diagrams"
- "really small system... then sorta try put everything together"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The student can articulate a components-first heuristic but still frames the overall process as insufficiently supported. His recommendation also evolves: he first proposes several small systems, then suggests that one evolving system may preserve context better.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
This aligns well with the lecturer's emphasis on worked examples and step-by-step guidance. The student perspective confirms that those forms of support are recognizable and desirable.

IMPLICATIONS:
Pedagogy or tools should externalise the process of modelling from text through examples, staged tasks, optional templates, and iterative feedback loops. An evolving case may combine gradual complexity with contextual continuity.

OPEN QUESTIONS:
Do these supports help students become independent, or do they risk over-structuring the activity? Which scaffold is most valuable first: examples, templates, or feedback timing?
