MEMO ID: S1M2
TITLE: Notation and tool overload are experienced together
DATE: 23/03/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 1
RELATED RQ(s): RQ1, RQ3
RELATED CODES: notation overload; notation feels arbitrary; overwhelming modelling tool; uncertainty about correct notation choice; preference for simpler tools; tools shape modelling difficulty
CATEGORY / THEME: Learning challenges / tooling

1. What is happening here?
The student says the main difficulty is not understanding high-level concepts, but dealing with notation, symbol choice, and overwhelming tools. Tool complexity and notation complexity are intertwined in the way he remembers the experience.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This suggests that students may not separate the formal language from the software environment used to construct it. If the tool is overwhelming, the notation itself may feel more arbitrary and difficult.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
There is a combined overload problem: too many symbols, too many interface options, and persistent doubt about whether the chosen relation is the correct one.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "different arrows meaning different things, and different boxes meaning different things"
- "it feels very arbitrary"
- "the tool that we used... had so many options"
- "is this the proper notation for this?"
- "having less things at my disposal makes it easier"
- "the UML one specifically made it harder, but drawio made it easier"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The simpler tool is preferred because it reduces overload, even though it may offer less semantic discipline than a modelling-specific environment.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
This is useful to compare directly with the lecturer memo on tooling. The lecturer values tools with checking and feedback; this student values simplicity and low overwhelm. These may pull in different design directions.

IMPLICATIONS:
Pedagogical tooling may need progressive disclosure or beginner-friendly modes so students are not confronted with full notation complexity all at once.

OPEN QUESTIONS:
Would students accept more advanced tool support if it were introduced gradually? How much complexity comes from notation versus interface design?
