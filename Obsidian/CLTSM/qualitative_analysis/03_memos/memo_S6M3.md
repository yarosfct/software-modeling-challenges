MEMO ID: S6M3
TITLE: NFR modelling turns qualities into traceable design reasoning
DATE: 09/06/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 6 (reconstructed)
RELATED RQ(s): RQ1, RQ2
RELATED CODES: examples connect quality attributes to design decisions; examples transform bullet qualities into modelled softgoals; quality-to-design traceability check; NFR modelling as judgement rather than proof
CATEGORY / THEME: Non-functional requirements and quality reasoning

1. What is happening here?
S6 learns that qualities such as security or performance are not simply listed. They must be represented as softgoals, connected to design decisions, and justified as partially satisfied.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This shows the semantic difficulty of NFR modelling: it requires judgement, argumentation, and traceability rather than direct structural representation.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Students need examples to move from declarative knowledge of quality attributes to modelling them as design-relevant rationale.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "how a quality attribute links to a softgoal and then to a design decision"
- "I would have listed qualities as bullet points rather than modelling them"
- "whether a design choice is traceable to a stated quality"
- "You cannot prove security in a diagram; you argue partial satisfaction"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Students may know quality attributes conceptually but not know how to model them. NFRs expose the limits of purely syntactic modelling instruction.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
L4 explicitly teaches NFR framework and quality refinements; S6 confirms that this area is difficult but meaningful when supported by examples and traceability.

IMPLICATIONS:
NFR instruction should include complete examples from quality concern to softgoal refinement to design decision, plus explanation of what is lost or only partially satisfied.

OPEN QUESTIONS:
Are NFRs difficult because they are abstract, because notation is unfamiliar, or because quality satisfaction is inherently argumentative?
