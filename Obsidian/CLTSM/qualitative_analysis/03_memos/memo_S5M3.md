MEMO ID: S5M3
TITLE: Granularity problems can appear as over-modelling, not only under-modelling
DATE: 09/06/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 5 (reconstructed)
RELATED RQ(s): RQ1, RQ2
RELATED CODES: sequence diagram complexity and granularity problem; not stuck starting but stuck refining detail; over-modelling tendency; examples calibrate granularity; contrastive examples teach simplification
CATEGORY / THEME: Abstraction and level of detail

1. What is happening here?
S5 does not struggle to start modelling. Instead, the student tends to create too much detail, especially in sequence diagrams, and needs examples to learn what to leave out.

2. Why is this analytically important?
It expands the category of modelling difficulty. Students may fail by producing too little, but they may also fail by over-specifying and losing the useful abstraction level.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
The issue is granularity judgement. Completeness can become harmful when students interpret it as including every message, loop, or detail.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "too many messages, optional fragments, and uncertainty about where to put loops"
- "I felt stuck on notation and granularity, not on starting"
- "I tend to over-model"
- "one bloated and one minimal"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Students are often told models should be complete, but also simple and readable. S5 shows how these criteria can pull in opposite directions.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
S4 also valued contrastive examples of too-detailed vs too-abstract models. Lecturer interviews repeatedly emphasise abstraction level and knowing when the model is enough.

IMPLICATIONS:
Courses should explicitly teach simplification and stopping criteria, perhaps through exercises where students improve bloated models rather than only create new ones.

OPEN QUESTIONS:
Which diagram types most often trigger over-modelling? Are sequence diagrams especially prone to excessive detail?
