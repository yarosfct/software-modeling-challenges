MEMO ID: L4M10
TITLE: AI can generate artefacts, but stakeholder communication stays human
DATE: 08/06/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview 4
RELATED RQ(s): RQ2, RQ3
RELATED CODES: assessment shift toward exams post-AI; human client communication irreplaceable; AI can transform specifications after human elicitation; abstraction struggle versus concrete execution preference
CATEGORY / THEME: AI / future of modelling education

1. What is happening here?
The lecturer has already shifted assessment toward exams because of AI's impact on take-home project trust. Regarding the future of the field, the lecturer argues that AI may generate code and low-quality models from specifications, but cannot replace communication with clients and stakeholders. Human requirements work remains central even as generation tools improve.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This connects AI to two teaching issues at once: academic integrity in assessment and the socio-technical identity of requirements engineering. AI intensifies the case for teaching communication and specification quality, not less.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
If generation becomes cheap, the scarce and valuable skills become problem understanding, elicitation, abstraction, justification, and client interaction. Students still prefer concrete, compilable outputs, but the labour market value may shift toward the human-facing parts AI does poorly.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "With artificial intelligence, things have changed... the exam has more weight"
- "requirements engineers and software engineers still have to communicate with the people who are paying for the product"
- "ChatGPT or Claude do not have that interaction with the client"
- "Once a requirement specification is built, then it can be given to an AI tool to transform into code or perhaps into models"
- "There are very good programmers who never attended university"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
AI pushes assessment back toward timed, guarded tasks, while the course philosophy values open discovery and iterative projects. Teachers must adapt evaluation without abandoning authentic requirements practice.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
Compared with lecturer interview 3, who framed LLMs as both disruption risk and a route to semantic feedback, L4 is more focused on irreplaceable stakeholder communication and exam-based assessment adaptation. Compared with student interview 3, who appreciated tool guidance but not AI-specific support, L4 sees AI as changing what must be human-led in modelling education.

IMPLICATIONS:
Modelling courses should emphasise elicitation, justification, and client-facing communication as AI-resistant competences, while reconsidering how project-based assessment can remain trustworthy.

OPEN QUESTIONS:
How should exams test communication-centred competences? Can AI feedback tools support rationale checking without replacing stakeholder interaction?
