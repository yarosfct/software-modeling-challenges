MEMO ID: S2M6
TITLE: The hardest part is representational choice, and students can imagine tools that support it
DATE: 23/03/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Student interview 2
RELATED RQ(s): RQ1, RQ2, RQ3
RELATED CODES: inherent modelling difficulty beyond tools; representational decision difficulty; endorsement of suggestive tool guidance; endorsement of guided checking tools; tool guidance more valuable at scale; digital tools preferred for speed and simplicity
CATEGORY / THEME: Tooling / desired supports

1. What is happening here?
The student says tools already make drawing easy, but the real difficulty lies in deciding what should be represented and how. When prompted, he endorses suggestions and checks for problematic relations, especially in larger models and database modelling.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This distinguishes low-level drawing support from higher-level modelling support. The student is effectively saying that usability is not enough; what matters is assistance with representational judgment.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
The hard part of modelling is conceptual placement and relation choice. Students may need support not only for making boxes and lines, but for deciding what those boxes and lines should mean.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "modelling has an inherent difficulty that tools cannot really simplify"
- "the hard part for me is thinking what I should put where"
- interviewer proposal: suggestions or checks about connections and relationships
- student response: "Yes, for sure"
- "An IDE that warned that a relationship did not make sense... would be very useful"
- "Especially for bigger models"
- "the online tools are already so good and simple and intuitive and fast"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The student first minimises the influence of existing tools, then endorses a hypothetical guided tool proposed by the interviewer. The contrast suggests that drawing support and semantic guidance are different tool categories, but the desired feature should be reported as prompted rather than spontaneous.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
This connects closely with the lecturer's desire for tools with feedback, checking, and next-step guidance. It also nuances student interview 1, where simple tools were preferred mainly to reduce overwhelm.

IMPLICATIONS:
There may be a productive design space for educational modelling tools that preserve simplicity while adding lightweight guidance around relation choice, validity, and abstraction decisions.

OPEN QUESTIONS:
What forms of guidance would students accept without feeling over-controlled? Would suggestions, examples, or warnings be the most useful intervention?
