TITLE: Modelling is framed as complexity management and traceability work
DATE: 09/04/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview 2
RELATED RQ(s): RQ3
RELATED CODES: modelling for complexity management; traceability across code and tests; abstraction and modularity for evolution; project-based cross-course assessment of competence
CATEGORY / THEME: Meaning of modelling / intended competence

1. What is happening here?
The lecturer presents modelling as a way to manage complexity, maintain abstraction, and preserve traceability across software artifacts. Models are not described as isolated diagrams, but as supports for development, change, and reuse.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This helps define what counts as modelling competence in this course. The intended outcome is not only notation use, but using models to think about systems over time and across artifacts.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
The lecturer sees modelling as valuable when it is connected to software development concerns such as code, tests, modularity, and evolution. That view is more practical and infrastructural than purely representational.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "they should be able to use models to manage complexity"
- "models are useful for traceability to code"
- "sometimes to tests"
- "modularity and abstraction, because systems change over time"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
The lecturer attributes strong practical value to modelling, but later also questions why industry does not seem to use modelling artifacts more often.

IMPLICATIONS:
Teaching and tool support may be stronger when modelling is explicitly linked to downstream software work, not presented only as formal diagram construction.

OPEN QUESTIONS:
Do students actually recognize traceability and change management as benefits, or is that mainly a lecturer rationale? Do projects make those links visible enough?
