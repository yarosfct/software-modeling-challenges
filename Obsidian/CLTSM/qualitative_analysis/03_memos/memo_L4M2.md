MEMO ID: L4M2
TITLE: Students arrive late with a code-first mindset
DATE: 08/06/2026
INTERVIEW / SOURCE: Lecturer interview 4
RELATED RQ(s): RQ1, RQ3
RELATED CODES: late-semester student arrival; broad Software Engineering prerequisite; programming-first student mindset; wish for earlier student access; students undervalue modelling; cannot compile this objection
CATEGORY / THEME: Contextual factors / prior exposure

1. What is happening here?
The lecturer gets students in the eighth semester with a broad but modelling-light Software Engineering background. Many arrive thinking they can simply program any solution and questioning the purpose of modelling because models do not compile. The lecturer repeatedly wishes students had earlier exposure to modelling.

2. Why is this analytically important?
This lecturer-student mismatch helps explain why advanced requirements teaching must fight entrenched implementation habits. The issue is not only missing notation knowledge but a whole mindset about what counts as real work in computer science.

3. What seems to be the underlying issue?
Modelling is introduced after students have formed strong identities as programmers and after a packed SE course has treated modelling as one topic among many. Executability becomes the default standard of value.

4. What evidence supports this?
- "They come to me quite late in their degree... in the eighth semester"
- "Software Engineering... is very broad"
- "What is this for? I can program a solution for whatever problem you give me"
- "Ideally, I would like access to students earlier"
- "What does this mean? I cannot compile this"

5. What tensions / contradictions appear?
Students do use abstraction in programming, but the lecturer says they may not realise it. So some required competence exists implicitly while modelling is rejected explicitly.

6. How does this relate to other interviews or literature?
Compared with student interviews 1 and 3, who also encountered modelling relatively late or in limited courses, L4 confirms the curricular timing problem from the teaching side. Compared with lecturer interview 3, who taught modelling in the second year, L4 shows what happens when requirements modelling is deferred to master's level.

IMPLICATIONS:
Earlier and lighter modelling exposure, even inside programming courses, may reduce the code-first barrier before advanced RE courses begin.

OPEN QUESTIONS:
Do students who meet iStar or requirements work earlier show less resistance? How much of the problem is timing versus course philosophy?
