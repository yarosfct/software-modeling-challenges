# Response-Mode Traceability Matrix

## Purpose

This matrix connects the 67 substantive memos to the four learner-response modes in the provisional axial model and records familiar-pattern reuse as a cross-cutting strategy.

Each memo receives:

- One **primary classification**
- Optional **secondary modes**
- Optional pattern-reuse markers
- A short rationale grounded in actions described by the memo

`Contextual only` means that the memo primarily describes a condition, support mechanism, intended competence, or outcome rather than direct evidence of a learner response.

## Mode Key

| Code | Response mode |
|---|---|
| DA | Defer to authority |
| EJA | Explore and justify alternatives |
| NC | Negotiate collaboratively |
| AAM | Avoid or abandon modelling |
| CO | Contextual only |

The modes describe situated responses, not fixed learner types.

Authority-deference variants:

| Code | Variant |
|---|---|
| DA-C | Calibration seeking after or during an independent attempt |
| DA-P | Paralysing dependence that prevents action without authority initiation or approval |
| DA-M | Mixed or shifting evidence across both variants |

The variants form a continuum and may change with notation familiarity, task complexity, feedback quality, and assessment risk.

Pattern-reuse markers:

| Code | Cross-cutting strategy |
|---|---|
| RPR | Reflective pattern reuse |
| UPR | Unreflective pattern reuse |

Pattern reuse can occur inside any response mode. It is reflective when learners understand, adapt, and evaluate a familiar structure; it is unreflective when they copy surface features without checking rationale or fit.

## Interpretation of the Distribution

- Lecturer memos predominantly describe conditions, intended competence, pedagogy, assessment, and tools; therefore, many are appropriately contextual rather than direct response evidence.
- Student memos provide most direct evidence of response modes.
- `Explore and justify alternatives` is the most common primary student response in the current later interviews, particularly S4-S6.
- Familiar-pattern reuse appears across response modes and varies between reflective adaptation and unreflective imitation.
- Authority deference ranges from productive calibration seeking to paralysing dependence and often shifts with assessment risk or notation familiarity.
- `Negotiate collaboratively` is concentrated in cases with shared ownership, trusted peers, stakeholder interaction, or public critique.
- `Avoid or abandon modelling` is strongly associated with weak purpose, implementation-first group cultures, framework burden, and overload.

These counts indicate traceability, not prevalence in a statistical sense. Memo numbers and interview depth differ across cases.

## Analytic Cautions

- A memo may contain several incidents that would receive different mode codes at excerpt level.
- Lecturer observations of student behaviour remain lecturer interpretations.
- Reconstructed student cases S4-S6 require provenance caution when used as evidence.
- Contextual memos should later be linked to the modes they enable or inhibit rather than converted into action evidence.
- The matrix should be revised if source-level comparison contradicts a memo-level assignment.
