# Negative-Case Test: Domain Choice and Engagement

## Initial Claim

Authentic and personally meaningful domains can increase motivation, effort, and perceived modelling value.

## Supporting Evidence

### Student cases

- S1 reports greater effort when projects feel authentic and consequential.
- S4 starts faster and refines more carefully when interested in the domain.
- S5 describes a relatable campus application as making modelling feel real.
- S6 values a bounded organisational domain with realistic stakeholder concerns.

## Negative and Limiting Cases

### Assessment pressure can make freedom undesirable

L4 observes that students under heavy evaluation pressure may prefer prescribed tasks. Open domains require:

- Scoping
- Requirement discovery
- More decisions
- Greater ambiguity tolerance

When time and cognitive capacity are limited, freedom can become an additional burden rather than a motivator.

### Domain choice creates unequal complexity

Lecturers L2-L4 identify problems including:

- Different amounts of domain knowledge
- Uneven project scope
- Incomparable modelling difficulty
- Different stakeholder access
- Greater risk of unmanageable topics

A student may appear more competent because the selected domain is easier or more familiar.

### Large courses require comparability

L3 uses a shared domain to support:

- Fair grading
- Reusable teaching support
- Common feedback
- Complexity control
- Integrity and copying management
- Feasible lecturer workload

### Choice increases teaching labour

Different domains require lecturers to understand:

- Distinct concepts and terminology
- Different scope risks
- Domain-specific assumptions
- Varied stakeholder arrangements

The motivational benefit of choice may not be feasible at large scale.

## Refined Interpretation

> Domain choice supports engagement only when choice is bounded, scope is controlled, and students have sufficient time and domain access; otherwise, freedom can increase uncertainty and inequality.

Choice is therefore a course-design resource with enabling conditions, not a universal recommendation.

## Bounded Choice Mechanism

Bounded choice may include:

- A limited set of approved domains
- A shared structural problem with optional contexts
- Scope and complexity criteria
- Required access to relevant information or stakeholders
- Lecturer approval before modelling begins
- Common modelling deliverables and quality criteria
- A default domain for students who prefer structure

This preserves some personal relevance while controlling assessment and support demands.

## Interaction with Course Context

### Large introductory courses

Likely needs:

- Common or tightly bounded domains
- Explicit domain orientation
- Comparable task structure
- Limited optional variation

### Small advanced courses

Can support:

- Wider student choice
- Stakeholder discovery
- More open requirements
- Domain-specific feedback
- Negotiated project scope

### High-workload contexts

Students may benefit from:

- Clear defaults
- Reduced domain-discovery burden
- Early scope confirmation
- Predictable milestones

## Distinguishing Engagement from Learning

Greater interest may increase:

- Speed
- Time invested
- Willingness to refine
- Perceived relevance

It does not automatically improve:

- Semantic correctness
- Abstraction quality
- Cross-model consistency
- Transfer to unfamiliar domains

The thesis should avoid treating motivation as equivalent to competence.

## Revised Proposition

> Personally meaningful domains can increase engagement and care, but their educational effect depends on bounded scope, equitable access to domain knowledge, manageable assessment conditions, and available teaching capacity. Shared domains may better support fairness and scalability, while bounded choice can preserve some authenticity and ownership.

## Implications

- Match domain policy to cohort size and course level.
- Provide a default domain alongside optional choice.
- Evaluate modelling competence separately from prior domain expertise.
- Supply necessary background information.
- Use scope gates and common quality criteria.
- Avoid assuming that all students prefer autonomy under assessment pressure.

## Remaining Tests

- Compare interest, effort, and model quality separately.
- Identify whether bounded choice produces stronger engagement than one common domain.
- Examine hidden inclusion assumptions in supposedly familiar domains.
- Compare lecturer workload across domain policies.
- Test whether domain transfer improves after varied examples.
