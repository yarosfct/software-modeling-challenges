# Local saturation assessment (category set)

Date: 2026-09-07. Comparison: [[wave2_constant_comparison]]. Open questions: [[open_questions_status]]. Canvas: [[canvases/local_saturation]].

This note is the audit trail for a **bounded** stopping claim. Methodology: STGT (Hoda) as used in `2-MainMatter/chapter4.tex`; Dey’s **theoretical sufficiency** as a hedge against overclaiming “saturation.”

---

## Claim (what we argue)

**Local saturation of the overview category set**, and **theoretical sufficiency** for this thesis’s RQs and sample.

Wave 2 (S7–S10: 50 codes, 18 memos) did **not** produce a 15th overview category that meets the existing warrant (≥2 memos, ≥2 cases, and will not fit an existing CAT). The 14 CAT / 4 PH set from wave 1 **held**. New interviews added **properties and bounds**, not new CAT labels.

That is the coordinator-facing fact: **later interviews did not result in new categories.**

---

## What we do not claim

- **Global theoretical saturation.** The student corpus is mostly Portuguese HE (IST, ISEL, plus earlier Portuguese cases), with two Wrocław students (S4, S5) and one Munich student (S6). Wave 2 added no new countries and **no new lecturers**.
- Saturation of **every property** of every CAT. CAT5 rework, CAT6 gating, CAT14 translation, and CAT13’s Portuguese incident **did** change the property list.
- Saturation of **lecturer-side** categories (CAT11 supply, CAT1 lecturer_interp, CAT5 designed-but-truncated loops). Those were not re-sampled.
- That S9 was a strong test (weak recall; excluded from CAT9/CAT13/CAT14).
- That unfulfilled open questions (abstraction deficit; lecturer “different questions” as syllabus content) are closed.

## Why global saturation is out of reach (document, don’t hide)

To saturate the **same** categories across “students and lecturers in modelling courses worldwide” would require many more programmes, notations, and countries than this master’s timeline and recruitment allowed. Wave 2 stayed inside Portuguese student variation (IST/ISEL) that the open questions actually needed. Two Polish and one German student sit in wave 1; they are not a global sample.

Transferability for the thesis remains **analytical** (conditions under which a CAT appears), as already stated in chapter 4 — not statistical generalisation.
